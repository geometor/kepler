About¶

Extracting Historical Semantic Knowledge & Repurposing It for Exact Symbolic Reasoning

About This Edition¶

geometor-kepler is a bilingual Digital Scriptorium Book Codex, semantic knowledge graph, and exact symbolic modeling environment dedicated to the foundational geometric and cosmological works of Johannes Kepler (1571–1630). Spanning three seminal treatises—Mysterium Cosmographicum (1596 / 1621), Strena seu de Nive Sexangula (On the Six-Cornered Snowflake, 1611), and Harmonices Mundi Libri V (The Harmony of the World, 1619)—this edition recovers Kepler’s revolutionary vision of geometry as the eternal archetypal blueprint of the physical universe.

The edition structures Kepler’s Latin prose into an interactive four-track Book Codex powered by photon-platform/publish. Every unit offers synchronized reading across English (freshly translated from early modern Latin imprints), Latin (verbatim critical text from Christian Frisch’s Opera Omnia), Summary (concise mathematical synopses of propositions and proofs), and Notes (in-depth philological, historical, and mathematical commentary). Key geometric concepts—from the Euclidean knowability (scibilitas) of regular constructible polygons to the irreducible non-constructibility (inscibilitas) of the heptagon, the classification of the 11 uniform Archimedean planar tilings, the discovery of the rhombic dodecahedron and triacontahedron, and Kepler’s Third Law—are formalized into machine-addressable semantic elements.

Every construction is grounded symbolically through geometor.model using exact algebraic fields rather than numerical approximations. Kepler’s famous golden right triangle $(1, \sqrt{\phi}, \phi)$ is formalized over the algebraic number field $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\phi})$ defined by $x^4 - x^2 - 1 = 0$, while polyhedral insphere/circumsphere ratios and close-packing densities ($\frac{\pi}{3\sqrt{2}}$) are verified symbolically with SymPy. This positions geometor-kepler at the vital crossroads of the GEOMETOR ecosystem, directly connecting the axiomatic foundations of geometor.elements with the harmonic proportions of geometor.divine and the morphology of geometor.pappus.

The GEOMETOR Mission¶

The GEOMETOR initiative, conceived and directed by phi ARCHITECT, is dedicated to the systematic recovery, symbolic formalization, and creative re-articulation of humanity’s geometric heritage. GEOMETOR unites classical synthesis with modern algebraic rigor across three core pillars:

Extracting Historical Semantic Knowledge

Moving beyond passive page scans, static facsimile bitmaps, and flat optical character recognition (OCR), GEOMETOR recovers the living deductive architecture of 2,300+ years of geometric, axiomatic, and philosophical treatises. Primary sources—spanning Euclid, Apollonius, Archimedes, Pappus, Johannes Kepler, Felix Klein, and Clark Kimberling—are analyzed to elevate enunciations, deductive steps, synthetic justifications, marginal citations, primary-source folios (via publish folio), and calibrated Ink-to-Vector .euclid-graphic diagrams into structured, machine-addressable semantic graphs.

Exact Symbolic Grounding (geometor.model)

Geometric constructions must not depend on floating-point approximations or visual artifacts. GEOMETOR anchors geometric entities in 100% exact symbolic algebra via SymPy and the principles of Rational Trigonometry (quadrance, spread, cross-ratio). Points, lines, circles, and conic loci are evaluated over exact algebraic fields, ensuring that tangencies, incidences, harmonic conjugates, and collinearities represent absolute mathematical truths rather than numerical estimates.

Repurposing Knowledge Across Mediums

Extracted semantic architectures and exact symbolic models are re-deployed across a unified digital ecosystem: synchronized multi-track Book Codices and EPUB 3 editions powered by photon-platform/publish, computable deductive canons (geometor.elements, geometor.etc, geometor.pappus, geometor.divine), interactive SVG exploration laboratories (geometor.explorer), ultra-high-definition visual broadcasts, and neuro-symbolic program synthesis (geometor.seer) across geometor.com.

Sources, Provenance & Methodology¶

Public-Domain Textual Provenance & Copyright Audit¶

All primary Latin texts in this repository derive exclusively from 100% Public Domain early modern imprints and nineteenth-century critical editions:

  1. *Mysterium Cosmographicum* (1596 / 1621): Prodromus Dissertationum Cosmographicarum, continens Mysterium Cosmographicum (Tübingen: Georg Gruppenbach, 1596; 2nd author-annotated ed., Frankfurt: Erasmus Kempfer / Gottfried Tampach, 1621), together with Kepler’s October 1597 letter to Michael Mästlin, edited by Christian Frisch in Joannis Kepleri Astronomi Opera Omnia, Vol. I (Frankfurt & Erlangen: Heyder & Zimmer, 1858, pp. 95–214, 377).

  2. *Strena seu de Nive Sexangula* (1611): Ioannis Kepleri S. Cæs. Maiest. Mathematici Strena seu de Nive Sexangula (Frankfurt am Main: Gottfried Tampach, 1611, 24 pp.), edited by Christian Frisch in Opera Omnia, Vol. VII (1868, pp. 715–730).

  3. *Harmonices Mundi Libri V* (1619): Ioannis Keppleri Harmonices Mundi Libri V (Linz: Gottfried Tampach / Johannes Planck, 1619), edited by Christian Frisch in Opera Omnia, Vol. V (1864, pp. 75–334).

Every English translation (-en), executive summary (-summary), and historical-mathematical commentary (-notes) in this codex is 100% originally translated and authored for GEOMETOR directly from Kepler’s Neo-Latin. Modern copyrighted translations—including C. G. Wallis (1939/1952), Colin Hardie (1966), A. M. Duncan & E. J. Aiton (1981), and E. J. Aiton, A. M. Duncan, & J. V. Field (1997)—were strictly excluded.
