Letter to Mästlin (1597): The Kepler Right Triangle¶

If upon a straight line cut in extreme and mean ratio a right-angled triangle be constructed out of the parts and the whole, in such a way that the side subtending the right angle is the greater part or the whole, then the square of one side together with the square of the other composes the square of the subtending side, and the three sides are continuously proportional.

Let us set down the unit base OA (\(= 1\)) between the points O and A, extend the straight line to W (\(-1, 0\)) and by the divine section (Euclid II.11) to P (\(\phi, 0\)). Having described the semicircle cThales upon the diameter WP, let the perpendicular erected through O at the right angle angAOB cut the circumference in B, so that OB \(= \sqrt{\phi}\) (Euclid VI.13).

Thus the right-angled triangle OAB (Kepler Right Triangle) has its three sides OA, OB, and AB in continuous geometric proportion (\(1 : \sqrt{\phi} : \phi\)), and their quadrances are

\[Q(\text{OA}) = 1, \qquad Q(\text{OB}) = \phi, \qquad Q(\text{AB}) = 1 + \phi = \phi^2;\]

and the perpendicular OH dropped from the right angle upon the hypotenuse AB at H divides it once more in extreme and mean ratio (\(BH = 1\), \(HA = \phi - 1\)).
