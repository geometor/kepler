Chapter III, Prop. VIII: Summary¶

Kepler’s Third Harmonic Law ($T^2 = a^3$)¶

In Harmonices Mundi Book V, Chapter III, Proposition VIII—conceived on March 8, 1618, initially rejected after a calculation slip, and triumphantly recovered on May 15, 1618—Kepler formulates the universal mathematical law (Lex Tertia) linking planetary orbital periods to mean heliocentric distances: the squares of the periodic times are proportional to the cubes of the semi-major axes:

$$\left(\frac{T_1}{T_2}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{a_1}{a_2}\right)^3, \qquad \text{or} \qquad T \propto a^{3/2}.$$

Thus if one planet were four times farther from the Sun than another ($a_1 : a_2 = 4 : 1$), its periodic time would be eightfold ($T_1 : T_2 = 8 : 1$, since $8^2 = 4^3 = 64$); if nine times farther ($9 : 1$), twenty-sevenfold ($27 : 1$, since $27^2 = 9^3 = 729$). Returning after twenty-two years to the five regular solids of Mysterium Cosmographicum (1596), armed with Tycho Brahe's observations and the focal ellipse of Astronomia Nova (1609), Kepler bridges polyhedral spacing and dynamical periods in preparation for the harmonic eccentricity proofs of Chapter IX.
