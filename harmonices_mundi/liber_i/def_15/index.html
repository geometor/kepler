Definition XV: Fourth Degree (Mese)¶

The degrees that follow are all called Ἄλογοι, Inexpressible. Latin translators have rendered this as Irrationales, at great peril of ambiguity and absurdity. Let us bury this usage of the word, because many lines, though Inexpressible, are nevertheless governed by the finest ratios. Arithmeticians by a like translation call them Surd Numbers, that is, numbers that speak no more than a deaf man hears; yet under this name they understand both quantities expressible in power only and inexpressible quantities.

Therefore the fourth degree in order—but the first of the inexpressibles—occurs when neither the line nor its square is Expressible, yet its square can be transformed into such a Rectangle whose sides are Expressible at least in power. This line is called Mese, because it is a mean proportional between two lines Expressible and commensurable in power only: as when one is indeed Expressible in length and the other in power only, or when both are Expressible in power only, yet their powers are not to one another as a square number to a square number.

Such a line is not known or measured in length by certain equal parts of the diameter, nor its square by the square of the diameter; nor are both of the lines between which Mese is the mean proportional measured together by the Diameter, but the squares of those lines—these at last are measured by the square of the diameter.

The square of a Mese is itself also called Meson, whether it be of square form or transformed into a Rectangle; and this is the second kind of Plane, after the Expressible plane: and by these two planes, the Expressible and the Meson, the following species are distinguished from one another.
