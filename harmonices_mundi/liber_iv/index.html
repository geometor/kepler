Book IV: Metaphysical, Psychological & Astrological Harmonies¶

Harmonices Mundi Liber IV: Metaphysicus, Psychologicus & Astrologicus, de Harmoniarum Generibus & Configurationibus Stellarum (Linz, 1619)

Outline of Book IV¶

Praeambulum: Metaphysical & Astrological Scope of Book IV

Opening Book IV (Metaphysicus, Psychologicus & Astrologicus), Kepler shifts from audible musical sound to the celestial rays descending upon Earth. Does harmony exist solely in vibrating strings, or do the planetary aspects—Conjunction, Opposition, Square, Trine, Sextile, Quintile, Biquintile—act upon sublunary souls and Earth’s atmosphere by purely geometric resonance?

Chapter I: Essence of Sensible and Intellectual Harmonies

Kepler distinguishes sensible harmony (material sounds or planetary rays colliding at a single recipient point or ear) from archetypal / intellectual harmony (the Soul’s comparison of two unequal quantities against the circle’s whole). Without an attentive mind (Anima) recognizing the proportion, external geometry remains silent.

Latin · English · Summary · Notes

Chapter II: Faculties of the Soul According to Harmonies

Chapter II classifies the vegetative, sensitive, and intellectual faculties of the Soul—and the World Soul / Earth Soul (Anima Telluris)—demonstrating how each faculty responds instinctively to pure circular proportions without conscious discursive calculation (just as a peasant feels rhythmic cadence or Earth trembles into storms under harmonic conjunctions).

Latin · English · Summary · Notes

Chapter III: Genera of Harmonized Beings (Sensible & Immaterial)

Before turning specifically to celestial aspects, Chapter III surveys all ten classes of harmonized things (τὰ ἡρμοσμένα) across geometry, arithmetic, music, light, and astronomy—showing how every domain mirrors the same underlying constructible polygons.

Latin · English · Summary · Notes

Chapter IV: Difference Between Book III Music & Book IV Planetary Aspects

Crucially, why do Book IV planetary aspects permit intervals such as Quintile ($72^\circ = \frac{1}{5}$), Biquintile ($144^\circ = \frac{2}{5}$), and Sesquiquadrate ($135^\circ = \frac{3}{8}$), whereas Book III rejects $1:5$ as a standalone melodic step? Because in music we divide a linear string (parts vs. parts), whereas on the zodiac two rays converge at Earth’s center, comparing each arc directly against the entire circle ($360^\circ$)!

Latin · English · Summary · Notes

Chapter V: Causes, Congruence, and Ranking of Efficacious Planetary Aspects

In the geometric heart of Book IV, Kepler explains why precisely eight celestial aspects are efficacious: Opposition ($180^\circ$, diameter), Trine ($120^\circ$), Square ($90^\circ$), Sextile ($60^\circ$), plus Kepler’s newly discovered Quintile ($72^\circ$), Biquintile ($144^\circ$), Semisextile ($30^\circ$), and Quincunx ($150^\circ$) / Octile-Trioctile ($45^\circ, 135^\circ$). Each arises when a central angle equals an inscribed polygon’s circumferential angle or complements a constructible polygon-star pair (Figura I–II and the pentagon/decagon/octagon congruence diagrams below).

Latin · English · Summary · Notes

Chapter VI: Kinship of Planetary Aspects with Musical Consonances

Chapter VI maps the two harmonics side by side: Conjunction ($0^\circ$) corresponds to Unison ($1:1$); Opposition ($180^\circ$) to Octave ($1:2$); Trine ($120^\circ$) to Fifth ($2:3$); Square ($90^\circ$) to Fourth ($3:4$); Sextile ($60^\circ$) to Minor Sixth/Third ($5:6$); and Quintile/Biquintile ($72^\circ, 144^\circ$) to the pentagonal consonances ($4:5, 3:5$).

Latin · English · Summary · Notes

Chapter VII: Epilogue on Sublunary Nature and Earth’s Meteoric Soul

Closing Book IV, Kepler examines sublunary nature—tides, meteorological storms, magnetic needles, plant phyllotaxis (pentagonal blossoms!), and human temperaments at birth—explaining how Anima Telluris (the Earth’s animating geometric faculty) answers celestial aspect angles while preserving human free will against superstitious horoscopy.
