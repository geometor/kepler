Definitions: Notes & Provenance¶

Editorial & Geometric Apparatus¶

  • Prooemium (p. 47–48): Connects Latin congruere / congruentia directly to Greek ἁρμόττειν / ἁρμονία (“fitting together”), grounding Book II’s geometric combinatorics in Kepler’s metaphysical architecture of Creation.

  • Space-Filling Solids & Honeycomb Cells (Def. V Nota, pp. 48–49): Kepler notes that among polyhedra filling 3D space around a point, only the cube and his newly described rhombus dodecahedricus (rhombic dodecahedron, which he links to the 3-rhombus base of bee honeycomb cells) achieve pure one-species space-filling—anticipating crystallography and Fedorov’s parallelohedra.

  • Infinite Prisms and Antiprisms (Def. X, p. 49): Classes A and B on the plate at fol. 53 refer respectively to regular \(n\)-gonal prisms (starting at \(n=3\), where \(n=4\) is the cube—a most perfect regular solid) and \(n\)-gonal antiprisms (starting at \(n=3\), the regular octahedron—another most perfect regular solid).

  • Why Local Diophantine Solutions Fail Globally (Def. XIII): A classic trap in elementary geometry is to assume that any set of regular polygons whose interior angles sum to \(360^\circ\) tiles the plane uniformly. Solving \(\sum (n_i - 2)/(2n_i) = 1\) yields 17 unordered multisets (and 21 cyclic vertex necklaces), yet 10 of those cyclic necklaces self-destruct after one or two steps! Consider Kepler’s counterexample 5.5.10 (\(108^\circ + 108^\circ + 144^\circ = 360^\circ\)): around any 5-sided pentagon, the polygons sharing its five edges would have to alternate 5, 10, 5, 10, ?—an odd cycle of length \(5\) cannot alternate two distinct symbols. Similarly, 3.7.42, 3.8.24, 3.9.18, 3.10.15, and 3.4.3.12 force neighbor collisions. In geometor.kepler.harmonices_geometry._is_globally_extendable_vertex, encoding Kepler’s exact odd-cycle walk condition eliminates all false local candidates automatically.

  • Print Erratum: In Def. VII (p. 49), the 1619 Linz edition prints regulatia for regularia. In Def. XI (p. 50), tueans is printed for tueatur (or tuens).

Transcription & Provenance¶

  • Latin Witness: Public-domain Neo-Latin text from Ioannis Keppleri Harmonices Mundi Liber II: Architectonicus, seu ex Geometria Figurata, de Figurarum Regularium Congruentia in Plano vel Solido (Linz, 1619, pp. 47–50; ed. Ch. Frisch, Vol. V, pp. 126–129).

  • English Track: Original GEOMETOR English translation from Johannes Kepler’s Harmonices Mundi Liber II (Linz: Tampach/Plancus, 1619, pp. 47–50; Frisch V, pp. 126–129).
