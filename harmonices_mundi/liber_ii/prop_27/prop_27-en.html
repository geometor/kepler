Proposition XXVII: The Two Rhombic Polyhedra¶

Semiregular plane figures—namely plane rhombi—also join in most perfect solid congruence, and in exactly two ways.

  1. The Rhombic Dodecahedron (Plate Vv): Twelve congruent plane rhombi whose diagonals stand in the ratio $\sqrt{2} : 1$ assemble into the solid rhombus—the exact form of the honeybee cell’s six-sided body and trihedral base. At its acute-angle vertices four rhombi meet; at its obtuse-angle vertices three rhombi meet.

  2. The Golden Rhombic Triacontahedron (Plate Xx): Thirty congruent plane rhombi whose diagonals stand in the Divine Section ratio $\phi : 1$ (PRgold to QSgold) form the thirty-faced solid rhombus. Five acute-angle rhombi join to cap two opposite polar cups, bound around the equator by a ten-rhombus belt.

Kepler proves that no third perfectly isohedral rhombic solid exists, since three obtuse angles meeting with three acute angles can only admit 4 or 5 acute angles around each acute vertex (with the Cube standing as the limiting principle of all rhombic solids where all angles become right).
