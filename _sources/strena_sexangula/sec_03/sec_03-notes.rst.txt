Sphere Close-Packing: Notes & Provenance
========================================

Equivalence of Kepler's Square-Pyramidal and Hexagonal Packings
---------------------------------------------------------------

A remarkable geometric subtlety appears in *Strena* §§11–12: Kepler describes **two** ways of stacking horizontal sphere layers so that each interior sphere touches twelve neighbors:

1. Start with a **square** grid in the horizontal :math:`(x, y)` plane and seat each sphere of the next layer into the fourfold hollow of four touching spheres below (cannonball pyramid).
2. Start with a **triangular-hexagonal** grid (`fig03`) and seat each sphere of the next layer into a threefold hollow (:math:`A \to B \to C \to A\dots` or :math:`A \to B \to A\dots`).

In fact, the square-based cannonball pyramid **is** the **face-centered cubic (FCC)** lattice viewed along its `[001]` fourfold axis, whose `[111]` diagonal planes are hexagonal layers in `ABCABC` stacking! Simple cubic stacking attains packing fraction :math:`\eta_{\text{cubic}} = \frac{\pi}{6} \approx 52.36\%`, whereas both `ABCABC` (FCC, Voronoi cell = **Rhombic Dodecahedron**) and `ABAB` (HCP, Voronoi cell = **Trapezo-Rhombic Dodecahedron**) achieve the exact maximum packing fraction :math:`\eta_{\text{FCC}} = \frac{\pi}{3\sqrt{2}} \approx 74.048\%`. After standing open for **387 years**, Kepler's optimality claim (*Coaptatio fiet arctissima, ut nullo praeterea ordine plures globuli in idem vas compingi queant*, p. 9) was proved by :person:`Thomas C. Hales <hales>` in 1998 (:cite:`published 2005 <hales:2005>`) and formally verified by machine in the Flyspeck theorem-proving project (2014). The contrasting :math:`3 \times 3` orthogonal cubic vapor-sphere lattice (p. 15, folio ``0037``) is presented alongside Kepler's discussion of octahedral snowflake condensation in :ref:`strena-sec_04`.
