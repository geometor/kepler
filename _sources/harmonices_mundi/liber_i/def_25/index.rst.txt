Definition XXV: Mizon, Elasson, and Origin of Euclid's Twelve Species
=====================================================================

.. folio:: harmonices_mundi:0031
   :page: p. 14

Therefore from the second pair of the sixth degree premised under Number XVIII—consisting of lines utterly incommensurable with one another, for which the sum of squares is Expressible and the Rectangle is *Meson*—by composition is born the :term:`Mizon <mizon-elasson>`, or Major; by subtraction, the *Elasson*, or Minor. From the third pair, where the sum of squares is *Meson* and the Rectangle is Expressible, the line born by composing has the name *Potens effabile et meson* (one whose square equals an expressible plus a medial area), and that born by subtracting, *Faciens cum effabili meson totum* (one which together with an expressible area makes the whole medial). Finally, from the fourth pair of the seventh degree premised under Number XIX, where each Effect is *Meson*, by composing arises *Potens bina media* (one whose square equals two medial areas), and by subtracting, *Faciens cum meso meson totum* (one which together with a medial area makes the whole medial).

And behold the Origin of the twelve Euclidean species, and the causes of their Number. For toward remoter ones—which carry either the sum of squares, or the common Rectangle, or both, beyond Expressibles and *Mesae* into lower species—Euclid did not judge that he ought to proceed.
