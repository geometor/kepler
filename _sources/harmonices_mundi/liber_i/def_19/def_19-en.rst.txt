Definition XIX: Seventh Degree of Knowledge
===========================================

.. folio:: harmonices_mundi:0029
   :page: p. 12

The **seventh, yet lower degree of knowledge** is when neither effect of two lines incommensurable with one another is expressible—neither the sum of their squares, nor their common Rectangle—yet both are still *Meson*.

Similar to these in effect are two *Mesae* commensurable in power only, of which one stands to the other as one of those (between which a *Mese* is truly the Mean proportional, commensurable namely in power only) stands to some third line commensurable in power only, by proposition 29 of the tenth book of Euclid.

These three pairs, distinguished by a twofold genus of Planes, Euclid teaches how to find chiefly because they serve for the composition and constitution of the species that follow.
