Proposition XXI: Closure of Length-Commensurable Pairs
======================================================

.. folio:: harmonices_mundi:0030
   :page: p. 12

**It must be understood, however, that from two lines commensurable with one another in length nothing arises that ought to be taken into account here—whether they be Expressible, or Mesae, or lower.**

For if they are commensurable in length, the whole compounded out of them will also be commensurable with its parts. Now whatever is commensurable with an Expressible line is Expressible,

.. folio:: harmonices_mundi:0031
   :page: p. 13

by the definitions before proposition 20 of the tenth book of Euclid. And whatever is commensurable with a *Mese* is a *Mese*, by 24 of the same. And whatever is commensurable with any of the Ineffables that are presently to follow after the *Mesae* is of the same species with it, by 66, 67, 68, 69, 70, 103, 104, 105, 106, and 107. And so it is likewise with other remoter species not mentioned by Euclid, which make remoter degrees. And even if it were not so with them, that nevertheless pertains nothing to us. For either they fall back into one of the species which we shall presently establish from incommensurables, and thus do not increase the number; or they produce lower species, either of their own or of another kind, and thus do not belong to this place, where we build degrees next in nobility to those premised.
