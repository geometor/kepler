Preface: Notes & Provenance
===========================

Why Harmonic Consonances Stop at the Number Five (*Senarius*)
-------------------------------------------------------------

Renaissance music theorists led by Gioseffo Zarlino (*Le Istitutioni Harmoniche*, 1558) had observed empirically that pure just-intonation musical consonances employ only ratios built from the integers $\{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$ (octave $1:2$, fifth $2:3$, fourth $3:4$, major third $4:5$, minor third $5:6$, major sixth $3:5$, minor sixth $5:8$), whereas **seven** ($6:7, 7:8$) sounds harsh and dissonant—yet Zarlino offered only numerological excuses (*senarius*).

In *Harmonices Mundi* Books I and III, :person:`Kepler <kepler>` replaced numerology with **constructive Euclidean geometry**: the soul judges harmonic proportions by comparing circular arcs against the whole circle through innate geometric archetypes (*scibilitas*). Because polygons of $3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12, 15, 16\dots$ sides are constructible by straightedge and compass (*Scibilia*), their arc divisions generate musical consonances; because the **regular heptagon** ($n = 7$), **enneagon** ($n = 9$), **hendecagon** ($n = 11$), and **tridecagon** ($n = 13$) are non-constructible (*Inscibilia*), the prime $7$ is barred from primary harmonic division.

Editorial & Archival Notes on the Prooemium
-------------------------------------------

* **Title Page (fol. 0019, p. [1])**: Carries the title *Io. Kepleri Harmonices Mundi Liber I. De Figurarum Regularium, quae Proportiones Harmonicas pariunt, ortu, classibus, ordine & differentiis, causa scientiae & Demonstrationis*, an epigraph from Proclus (*In I. Euclidis*), and the Linz 1619 imprint of Johannes Plancus.
* **Marginalia (fols. 0020–0023, pp. 2–5)**: Kepler's printed marginal rubrics mark the sections: *Proclus de intellectuali essentia rerum Geometricarum* (p. 2), *Petri Rami iniqua in Euclidem & imperita censura* (p. 3), *Lazarus Schonerus* (p. 3), *Lazari Schoneri sententia de quinque figuris solidis* (p. 4), *Pythagoreorum de quinque figuris Mystica interpretatio* (p. 4), *Willebrordi Snellij de Binominalibus sententia* (p. 4), and *Occasio hujus Libri I.* (p. 5).
* **Continuous Numbering**: Kepler notes on p. 6 that he numbered Definitions, Propositions, and Theorems in one continuous sequence (*I–L*), following his practice in the *Dioptrice* (1611), and advises non-mathematical readers to skip ahead to Proposition XXX through the end if desired.
