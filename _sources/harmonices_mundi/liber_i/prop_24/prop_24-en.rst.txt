Proposition XXIV: First Incommensurable Pair Reduces to Binomial/Apotome
========================================================================

.. folio:: harmonices_mundi:0031
   :page: p. 13

**Yet from the first pair of such utterly incommensurable lines—namely those that are knowable in the fifth degree under XVII premised above—by adding or subtracting them an Expressible line is born again; and they are necessarily a BINOMIAL and an APOTOME: see 112, 113, and 114 of the tenth book of Euclid. So that if both the sum of the squares of a Binomial and an Apotome and their common Rectangle be Expressible, each individual Name of the one must be commensurable with each individual Name of the other—which does not happen in all BINOMIALS and APOTOMES.**

That two such lines, producing the two required effects, must necessarily become

.. folio:: harmonices_mundi:0032
   :page: p. 14

a Binomial and an Apotome is demonstrated in the same manner in which 33 of the tenth book was demonstrated—only so that in place of two lines *expressible in power only* (ῥηταῖς δυνάμει μόνον), there be employed lines *expressible in length* (ῥηταὶ μήκει), and wherever the word *meson* (μέσον) occurs, *expressible* (ῥητὸν) be put; and finally let the Definition of Binomial and Apotome be compared.

Now that from the composition and subtraction of a Binomial and an Apotome producing the two required Effects, an Expressible line arises again, is evident thus. For since the sum of the squares is Expressible and the rectangle is Expressible, when they are compounded into one, the square will consist of the square of each and of the two common rectangles—that is, of two Expressible parts: wherefore the whole square will also be Expressible, and therefore the compounded line as well, which is potent in that square. Let $\lambda\mu$ be a Binomial and $\kappa o$ its square, and let $\lambda\theta$ be an apotome and $\theta\kappa$ its square; and let $\theta\kappa$, $\kappa o$ joined together be Expressible, and let the Rectangle of $\theta\lambda$, $\lambda\mu$ be expressible—now two of these, $\kappa\mu$ and $\kappa\xi$, fill up the square of the whole compounded line $\theta\mu$, which square is $\theta o$.

Concerning subtraction, it is proved in this manner. For if the line compounded of $\theta\lambda$, $\mu\lambda$—that is, $\theta\mu$—is expressible, then its half $\theta\pi$ will also be Expressible, as well as the greater Name, and $\pi\lambda$ the Lesser name, and the other half $\pi\mu$. Take away from it $\mu\sigma$ equal to $\theta\lambda$; then the remainder $\pi\sigma$ will also be Expressible, and the whole $\lambda\sigma$, namely double of $\pi\sigma$. Now $\pi\sigma$ is the remainder after taking away the Apotome $\mu\sigma$ from the Binomial $\lambda\mu$. The remainder therefore becomes Expressible.
