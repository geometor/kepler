Prooemium & Definitions I–XIII: Summary
=======================================

Planar & Solid Congruence Criteria
----------------------------------

In the *Prooemium* and Definitions I–XIII of *Harmonices Mundi* Book II (pp. 47–50), Kepler introduces **architectonic congruence**—the geometric sociability (*ἁρμόττειν*) of regular and star polygons:

* **Prooemium**: Contrasts *demonstrability* (Book I, which extends infinitely by doubling sides) with *congruence* (Book II, strictly bounded by angle-sum constraints), presenting geometric congruence as the archetype of cosmic and natural harmony.
* **Planar Congruence (Defs. I–IV)**: Angles meet around a vertex with no gap (:math:`360^\circ`); *perfect* when all vertices are uniform and extend to infinity; *most perfect* when all faces belong to a single species; *imperfect* when finite or mixed-vertex.
* **Solid Congruence (Defs. V–XI)**: Plane angles sum to less than :math:`360^\circ` to form a closed polyhedral corner (*most perfect* for Platonic and rhombic-semiregular solids; *perfect of a lower degree* for Archimedean solids; *imperfect* for prisms and antiprisms; *semisolid* for star/gapped polyhedra). Also notes space-filling by cubes and rhombic dodecahedra (honeycomb cells).
* **Congruent vs. Incongruent Figures (Defs. XII–XIII)**: Excludes the regular heptagon, 15-gon, and higher non-tiling polygons that yield only prisms/antiprisms or non-uniform planar patches.
