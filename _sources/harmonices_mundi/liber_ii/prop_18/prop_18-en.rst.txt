Proposition XVIII: Only Three Plane Figures Fill a Plane by One Species
=======================================================================

.. folio:: harmonices_mundi:0069
   :page: p. 51

**Proposition XVIII.**

*A plane place is most perfectly filled by planes of the same figure in three ways only: six triangles, four squares, and three hexagons.*

For by Proposition XXXIII of the First Book of this work, the angle of a triangle is two thirds of one right angle; six angles therefore of six triangles make twelve thirds, that is, four whole right angles. See *D*. Likewise the angle of a square is one right angle; four angles therefore of four squares make four right angles. See *E*. Likewise the angle of a hexagon is eight sixths of one right angle; three therefore of three figures make twenty-four sixths, that is, four right angles; see *F*. But the angle of a pentagon is smaller than that of a hexagon; three therefore are less than four right angles, and thus three leave a gap. The same angle is greater than that of a square; four pentagons therefore are greater than four right angles, and therefore are not contained in one plane place, by Proposition XVI of this book. For this reason see *H*, with the fourth pentagon marked by dotted lines. Likewise the angle of a heptagon, and of all larger figures, is greater than that of a hexagon; three heptagons therefore exceed four right angles. See *I*, where parts of two heptagons cover the same place in the plane.

Refer hither also the rhombi, each consisting of two regular triangles. For they are most perfectly congruent, like regular hexagons, even though they themselves are semiregular figures. See this congruence in the following copperplate diagram at letter *G*.

Refer hither also the six-pointed stars made from a dodecagon by cutting out six rays, at letter *K*: for into the place of the cut-out ray there comes a concave angle equal to a right angle. Therefore three square angles and three rays of such stars fill the place. For a hexagon is divided into such a star and six half-squares.
