Chapter IV: Consonances of Extremal Planetary Motions
=====================================================

.. folio:: harmonices_mundi:0284
   :page: pp. 192–202

In Chapter IV, Kepler asks *which physical quantities* of orbital motion the Creator tuned to musical consonance: not mean distances alone, nor linear orbital speeds, nor solar-center periods—rather **apparent daily heliocentric angular motions** ($\omega$) at **aphelion** (:g:`A_aph`) and **perihelion** (:g:`P_per`)! Because a planet sweeps equal focal sector areas around the Sun (:g:`Sol_f`) in equal times—comparing the short, wide perihelion sector :g:`SEC_PER` against the long, narrow aphelion sector :g:`SEC_APH`—the extremal daily angular speeds stand strictly in the inverse square of the extreme solar distances:

$$\frac{\omega_{\text{per}}}{\omega_{\text{aph}}} = \left(\frac{r_{\text{aph}}}{r_{\text{per}}}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{1 + e}{1 - e}\right)^2.$$

Comparing both converging and diverging extremal speeds of adjacent planets reveals pure harmonic ratios connecting every orbital boundary from Saturn down to Mercury.
