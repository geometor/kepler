Chapter VI: The Engraved Planetary Melodies & Celestial Music Studio
====================================================================

.. folio:: harmonices_mundi:0298
   :page: pp. 206–208

In the celebrated **Chapter VI**, Kepler assigns each individual planet its own sung melody (:term:`harmonia motuum extremorum <harmonia-extremorum>`), gliding continuously between its minimum speed at aphelion and maximum speed at perihelion:

* **Saturn** ($1'46'' : 2'15''$ per day $\approx 4 : 5$) — **Major Third** (*ditonus*);
* **Jupiter** ($4'30'' : 5'30''$ per day $= 5 : 6$) — **Minor Third** (*sesquiquinta*);
* **Mars** ($26'14'' : 38'1''$ per day $\approx 2 : 3$) — **Perfect Fifth** (*diapente*);
* **Earth** ($57'3'' : 61'18''$ per day $\approx 15 : 16$) — **Diatonic Semitone** (*mi–fa*, inspiring Kepler's famous epigram: *"Earth sings MI–FA–MI, so that even from the syllable you may guess that in this home of ours MIsery and FAmine hold sway"*);
* **Venus** ($94'50'' : 97'37''$ per day $\approx 24 : 25$) — **Chromatic Diesis**;
* **Mercury** ($164'0'' : 384'0''$ per day $= 5 : 12$) — **Octave plus Minor Third** (14-note sweeping arpeggio), accompanied marginally by the **Moon**.
