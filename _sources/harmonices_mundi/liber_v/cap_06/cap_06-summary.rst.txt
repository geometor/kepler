Chapter VI: Summary
===================

In Chapter VI, Kepler assigns each individual planet its own continuous melody (:term:`harmonia motuum extremorum <harmonia-extremorum>`), gliding without leaps between its minimum daily angular velocity at aphelion and its maximum daily angular velocity at perihelion, and provisionally associates each planet with traditional ecclesiastical musical modes:

* **Saturn** (:math:`1'46'' : 2'15''` per day :math:`\approx 4 : 5`) — **Major Third** (*ditonus*, :math:`G`–:math:`h`; Modes VII–VIII);
* **Jupiter** (:math:`4'30'' : 5'30''` per day :math:`= 5 : 6`) — **Minor Third** (*sesquiquinta*, :math:`G`–:math:`b\flat`; Modes I–II);
* **Mars** (:math:`26'14'' : 38'1''` per day :math:`\approx 2 : 3`) — **Perfect Fifth** (*diapente*, :math:`f`–:math:`c`; Modes V–VI);
* **Earth** (:math:`57'3'' : 61'18''` per day :math:`\approx 15 : 16`) — **Diatonic Semitone** (*mi–fa–mi*, :math:`G`–:math:`A\flat`/:math:`e`–:math:`f`; Modes III–IV), prompting Kepler's famous marginal epigram (*"Tellus canit MI FA MI..."*);
* **Venus** (:math:`94'50'' : 97'37''` per day :math:`\approx 24 : 25`) — **Chromatic Diesis** (practically unison; Modes III–IV by its place :math:`e` in the common system);
* **Mercury** (:math:`164'0'' : 384'0''` per day :math:`= 5 : 12`) — **Octave plus Minor Third** (14-note sweeping span; all modes), accompanied on the engraved score by the **Moon** (*"Hic locum habet etiam ☽"*).
