Letter to Mästlin (1597): The Kepler Right Triangle
===================================================

If upon a straight line cut in :term:`extreme and mean ratio <sectio-divina>` a right-angled triangle be constructed out of the parts and the whole, in such a way that the side subtending the right angle is the greater part or the whole, then the square of one side together with the square of the other composes the square of the subtending side, and the three sides are continuously proportional.

Let us set down the unit base :g:`OA` (:math:`= 1`) between the points :g:`O` and :g:`A`, extend the straight line to :g:`W` (:math:`-1, 0`) and by the :term:`divine section <sectio-divina>` (:cite:`Euclid II.11 <euclid:elements>`) to :g:`P` (:math:`\phi, 0`). Having described the semicircle :g:`cThales` upon the diameter :g:`WP`, let the perpendicular erected through :g:`O` at the right angle :g:`angAOB` cut the circumference in :g:`B`, so that :g:`OB` :math:`= \sqrt{\phi}` (:cite:`Euclid VI.13 <euclid:elements>`).

Thus the right-angled triangle :g:`OAB` (:term:`Kepler Right Triangle <kepler-triangle>`) has its three sides :g:`OA`, :g:`OB`, and :g:`AB` in continuous geometric proportion (:math:`1 : \sqrt{\phi} : \phi`), and their :term:`quadrances <quadrance>` are

.. math::

   Q(\text{OA}) = 1, \qquad Q(\text{OB}) = \phi, \qquad Q(\text{AB}) = 1 + \phi = \phi^2;

and the perpendicular :g:`OH` dropped from the right angle upon the hypotenuse :g:`AB` at :g:`H` divides it once more in extreme and mean ratio (:math:`BH = 1`, :math:`HA = \phi - 1`).
