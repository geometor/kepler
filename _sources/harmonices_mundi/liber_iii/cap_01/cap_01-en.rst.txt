Chapter I: Origin of Consonances from Constructible Polygons
============================================================

.. folio:: harmonices_mundi:0100
   :page: pp. 10–21

In Chapter I, Kepler states his **Seven Harmonic Axioms**: when a circle is divided by an inscribed constructible regular polygon, the resulting arc and its remainder stand in a consonant relation to the whole circle—and to one another—only when the polygon is *knowable* (:term:`scibilis <scibilitas>`, Book I). Thus the **Diameter** ($1:2$), **Triangle** ($1:3, 2:3$), **Square** ($1:4, 3:4$), **Pentagon** ($1:5, 2:5, 3:5, 4:5$), **Hexagon** ($1:6, 5:6$), and **Octagon** ($1:8, 3:8, 5:8$) generate the exact **5-limit Just-Intonation consonances**, while the **Heptagon** ($7$) is excluded for lack of geometric constructibility.
