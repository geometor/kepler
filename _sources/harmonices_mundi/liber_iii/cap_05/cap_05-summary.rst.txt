Chapter V: Summary
==================

Chapter V demonstrates how each primary consonance decomposes naturally into chains of melodic (*concinnous*) steps, grounding traditional interval nomenclature in 5-limit Just Intonation:

* **Fourth (*Diatessaron*, $3:4$)**: Divides into one major tone, one minor tone, and one diatonic semitone ($\frac{8}{9} \cdot \frac{9}{10} \cdot \frac{15}{16} = \frac{3}{4}$), spanning four voices.
* **Fifth (*Diapente*, $2:3$)**: Adds one major tone ($8:9$) to the Fourth; one intermediate step remains undivided until Book V's planetary motions supply it.
* **Thirds & Sixths (*Mollia* vs. *Dura*)**: $5:6$ and $5:8$ derive their softer character from the expressible hexagon and octave bisection, while $4:5$ and $3:5$ inherit the harsher inexpressible pentagon.
* **Octave (*Diapason*, $1:2$)**: Joins Fifth and Fourth around a shared central tone into seven concinnous steps across eight voices, verifying $(9/8)^3 (10/9)^2 (16/15)^2 = 2$ and excluding the *Diesis* ($24:25$) and *Limma* ($128:135$) from the principal diatonic scale.
