Chapter III: Primary and Secondary Solids & Earth's Station
===========================================================

.. folio:: mysterium_cosmographicum:0036
   :page: p. 26

Lest the nesting of the :term:`five regular solids <corpora-quinque-regularia>` between Copernicus's six spheres seem accidental, Kepler proves that the polyhedra themselves obey an intrinsic geometric hierarchy dictating their cosmic order. They divide naturally into two distinct ranks: **three primary solids** (*primaria*)—the **Cube**, **Tetrahedron** (*Pyramis*), and **Dodecahedron**—and **two secondary solids** (*secundaria*)—the **Octahedron** and **Icosahedron**.

Kepler enumerates seven mathematical marks separating the two orders:

1. **Distinct versus shared faces**: The primary solids differ mutually in their bounding polygons, whereas both secondary solids employ triangles.
2. **Face ownership**: Each primary solid owns a native face—the Cube the square, the Pyramid the triangle, the Dodecahedron the pentagon—while the secondary solids borrow the triangle from the Pyramid.
3. **Trihedral simplicity**: Every primary solid meets in the simplest possible solid angle of three planes, whereas secondary solids require four (Octahedron) or five (Icosahedron) planes per vertex.
4. **Ontological priority**: Primary solids owe their origin to none; secondary solids arise from primary ones by mutual interchange (*commutatio*, modern polar duality).
5. **Axis of rotation**: Primary solids rotate gracefully about axes passing through face centers; secondary solids rotate about axes joining opposite vertices.
6. **Posture (*stare* vs. *pendere*)**: Primary solids naturally *stand* upright upon a flat base, whereas secondary solids naturally *hang* suspended from a vertex.
7. **Triadic and angular completeness**: Primary solids number three and span all three Euclidean angle species—right (Cube), acute (Tetrahedron), and obtuse (Dodecahedron)—whereas secondary solids number two and possess only obtuse dihedral/face-junction angles (the Octahedron alone exhibiting obtuse, right, and acute aspects across its edges, cross-section, and solid vertex).

Between these two unmistakable ranks, nothing could be more fitting than to station Earth—summit and compendium of the created world—as the dividing orb.
