Chapter I: Notes & Provenance
=============================

Epistemology of Hypotheses & Folding Plates Tabella I–II
--------------------------------------------------------

* **Anti-Instrumentalism**: Kepler's refutation of the *ex falso quodlibet* reading (made famous by Andreas Osiander's anonymous preface to Copernicus's 1543 *De revolutionibus*) marks a decisive watershed in the philosophy of science. Even :person:`Tycho Brahe <tycho>`, though dissenting on Earth's motion, adopted Copernicus's insight that the Sun centers the five planetary orbits.
* **Tabella I (`0026`) vs. Tabella II (`0027`)**: By contrasting Copernican heliocentric orbs (:g:`cSAT`, :g:`cJUP`, :g:`cMAR`, :g:`cTER`, :g:`cVEN`, :g:`cMER` around :g:`Sol`, with tangent parallax cones from Saturn :g:`L`, Jupiter :g:`I`, and Mars :g:`G` to Earth's *Orbis Magnus* :g:`A`) against Ptolemaic epicycles (:g:`cSAT_ep`, :g:`cJUP_ep`, :g:`cMAR_ep`, :g:`cVEN_ep`, :g:`cMER_ep` and *Arcus prosthaphaereseon* :g:`arcSAT`, :g:`arcJUP`, :g:`arcMER`, :g:`arcMAR`, :g:`arcVEN` centered on :g:`Terra`), Kepler demonstrates why Copernicus uniquely fixes interplanetary distance ratios—the indispensable empirical precondition for fitting the :term:`five regular solids <corpora-quinque-regularia>` between the six orbs.
* **Textual & Editorial Notes**:
  * On fol. ``0023`` (p. 16), the 1596 *editio princeps* prints ``sub angulo TAV`` for Mars's parallax cone, a typographical slip for ``TGV`` (vertex :g:`G` in Tabella I).
  * On fol. ``0024`` (p. 17), the 1596 print includes a small marginal figure-eight / twisted-wreath diagram (*corolla intorta*) illustrating the double libration of the terrestrial poles.
  * For readers lacking leisure to read *De revolutionibus* in full, Kepler recommends :person:`Rheticus's <rheticus>` *Narratio Prima*, appended by :person:`Mästlin <maestlin>` to the 1596 Tübingen volume.
