Chapter I: Summary
==================

Physical Realism & The Heliocentrism Defense
--------------------------------------------

Chapter I is Kepler's foundational manifesto of astronomical realism. Before unfolding the polyhedral secret of the cosmos, he establishes why the heliocentric hypotheses of :person:`Nicolaus Copernicus <copernicus>` (:cite:`De revolutionibus, 1543 <copernicus:1543>`) deserve full physical assent rather than dismissal as a mere calculating fiction:

1. **Refutation of *ex falso quodlibet* instrumentalism**: Rejecting the scholastic claim that false premises can perpetually yield true celestial appearances, Kepler distinguishes accidental syllogisms from systematic physical principles and diagnoses Ptolemy's error as confusing species with genus (*kata auto*).
2. **Authority of Mästlin & Rheticus**: Kepler credits his teacher :person:`Michael Mästlin's <maestlin>` superlunary observations of the 1577 comet in the Copernican orb of Venus, and recommends :person:`Georg Joachim Rheticus's <rheticus>` *Narratio Prima* (:cite:`1540 / 1596 <rheticus:1540>`).
3. **Unification of Copernicus's four terrestrial motions**: Across folding plates **Tabella I** (`0026`) and **Tabella II** (`0027`), Kepler shows how Earth's annual *Orbis Magnus*, eccentricity variation, equatorial axial motion, double polarmotion libration, and 24-hour diurnal rotation banish eleven redundant Ptolemaic circles, explain retrograde arcs, planetary brilliance, and bounded inner elongations, and fix commensurable interplanetary distances.
