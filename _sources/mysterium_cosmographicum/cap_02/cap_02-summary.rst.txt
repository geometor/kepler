Chapter II: Summary
===================

Curved, Straight & The Solid Angle Obstruction
----------------------------------------------

In Chapter II, :person:`Kepler <kepler>` invokes :person:`Nicholas of Cusa <cusanus>` to establish the fundamental cosmological polarity between the spherical universe (*Curvum*, likeness of God) and interior polyhedral boundaries (*Rectum*, likeness of creatures). Setting aside the innumerable fixed stars, he turns to the six moving planets around the Sun and asks why there are precisely six orbs with their observed proportions.

By :person:`Euclid's <euclid>` *Elements* XIII.18 scholium, solid-angle closure requires plane face angles meeting at a vertex to sum to strictly less than four right angles (:math:`360^\circ`), admitting precisely the five Platonic solids:

* Three regular equilateral triangle vertices (:math:`180^\circ, 240^\circ, 300^\circ`): **Tetrahedron** (between Jupiter and Mars), **Octahedron** (between Venus and Mercury), **Icosahedron** (Earth–Venus or Mars–Earth);
* One square vertex (:math:`270^\circ`): **Cube** (between Saturn and Jupiter);
* One regular pentagon vertex (:math:`324^\circ`): **Dodecahedron** (Mars–Earth or Earth–Venus).

Because five regular polyhedral intervals require six bounding spherical orbs, Kepler accounts *a priori* for the six Copernican planetary spheres and illustrates their nesting in **Tabula III**.
