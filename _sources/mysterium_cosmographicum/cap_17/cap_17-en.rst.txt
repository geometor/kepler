Chapter XVII: Mercury's Inscribed Square in the Octahedron
==========================================================

.. folio:: mysterium_cosmographicum:0068
   :page: p. 56

Kepler confronts a glaring technical exception in his own tables of Chapters XIII and XIV: although he promised to inscribe each planet within the **insphere** of its surrounding polyhedron, for **Mercury** inside the **Octahedron** he replaced the true insphere radius ($1000 / \sqrt{3} \approx 577$) with the much larger radius of the **circle inscribed in the Octahedron's equatorial square** ($1000 / \sqrt{2} \approx 707$).

Why is this exception geometrically unique and astronomically forced? First, Mercury's remarkably large orbital eccentricity and solar elongation simply refuse to fit inside the narrow prison of $577$. Second—recalling his geometric discovery from Chapter IX—the **Octahedron alone among all five Platonic solids**, when erected upon two opposite vertices ($X, H$ in Kepler's diagram on p. 56), possesses four continuous edges forming an unobstructed **equatorial square** perpendicular to the polar axis, creating a wider circular clearance around its middle than its true insphere allows.

In the woodcut diagram on folio ``0068`` (p. 56), Kepler draws the Octahedron's vertical cross-section :g:`< X S H Q >` (vertices :g:`X`, :g:`S`, :g:`H`, :g:`Q` about center :g:`O`), where the four bounding segments are the face altitudes of four octahedral triangles and :g:`R`, :g:`V`, :g:`T`, :g:`I` mark their face centers, defining the true inscribed sphere :g:`( O P )` of radius :g:`/ O I /` = :g:`/ O P /` = 577:

$$\frac{OP}{OX} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \approx 0.57735, \qquad \frac{OQ}{OX} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \approx 0.70711,$$

leaving the excess clearance strip :g:`/ P Q /` ($= OQ - OP$) around the equator—like the horizon ring of an armillary sphere—within which Mercury's tilted and highly eccentric orbit ranges up to radius $474$ (compared against Copernicus's $488$ aphelion and $360$ mean distance). Kepler invites expert astronomers to examine whether Mercury's poorly observed motion requires revising its eccentricity or confirming this unique octahedral equatorial belt.
