Chapter IV: Why Three Solids Surround Earth and Two Lie Within
==============================================================

.. folio:: mysterium_cosmographicum:0037
   :page: p. 27

Having stationed Earth between the primary and secondary orders, Kepler next asks why the **three primary solids** surround Earth's orb on the outside while the **two secondary solids** are enclosed within it—rather than the reverse arrangement. Asking the reader's indulgence for a brief theological reflection, he notes that humanity is the contemplative crown of creation, and therefore Earth—dwelling place of the Creator's image—was fitted to circle midway among the celestial bodies so as to have an equal triad of bodies outside and inside its circuit.

To achieve this three-and-three symmetry, the Creator numbered the stationary Sun together with the two interior planets (Venus and Mercury), yielding three bodies inside Earth's orb to balance the three superior planets (Mars, Jupiter, Saturn) outside it—while the Moon accompanies Earth itself, restoring the venerable septenary count around humanity's home. Had the triad of primary solids been placed inside Earth's orb, four bodies (including the Sun) would have been crowded within and only two left without—a numerical disorder (*ataxia*) avoided by the Architect.

Furthermore, a strictly formal principle confirms the same order: **to contain** (*continere*) belongs to the more active and perfect, whereas **to be contained** (*contineri*) belongs to the passive and secondary. Since the three primary solids surpass the two secondary ones in geometric priority, the primary triad rightfully encloses Earth's sphere, while the secondary pair lies inscribed within it. Yet Kepler candidly remarks that even if a reader treats this teleological reason as supplementary, the astronomical fact itself is uncontested: in Copernicus three planets revolve above Earth and two below, requiring Cube, Tetrahedron, and Dodecahedron outside Earth's orb and Icosahedron and Octahedron within.
