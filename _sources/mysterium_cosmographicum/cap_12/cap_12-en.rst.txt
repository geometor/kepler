Chapters XII–XIII: The Two Treasures of Geometry & Polar Duality
================================================================

*Original GEOMETOR English translation from Johannes Kepler's Mysterium Cosmographicum, Capita XII–XIII* (:cite:`Tübingen, 1596 <kepler:1596:mc>`; :cite:`Frankfurt, 1621 <kepler:1621:mc2>`; :cite:`Frisch I, pp. 138–143 <frisch:1858:v1>`).

Geometry possesses **two great treasures**: the one is the ratio of the hypotenuse to the legs in a right-angled triangle (:just:`I.47`), and the other is the straight line cut in extreme and mean ratio—the :term:`Divine Section <sectio-divina>` (:just:`II.11`, :just:`VI.30`). The first you may rightly compare to a **measure of gold** (*massa auri*); the second we may call a **precious jewel** (*gemma*). For from the first flows the construction of the **Cube**, the **Tetrahedron** (*Pyramis*), and the **Octahedron**; whereas from the second—self-propagating and ineffable—flows the construction of the **Dodecahedron** and the **Icosahedron** (:cite:`Pacioli 1509 <pacioli:1509>`).

When each regular solid is inscribed in a unit outer sphere ($R_{\text{out}} = 1$, $Q_{\text{out}} = 1$) so that its vertices touch the outer planet's inner boundary while an inner sphere ($r_{\text{in}}$, $Q_{\text{in}} = r_{\text{in}}^2$) touches the centers of its faces, exact Euclidean demonstration yields three distinct :term:`quadrance <quadrance>` ratios, because **polar-dual** solids share identical insphere-to-circumsphere proportions:

* **Cube & Octahedron** ($\{4, 3\}$ and $\{3, 4\}$):

  $$\frac{r_{\text{in}}}{R_{\text{out}}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{3} \approx 0.57735, \qquad \frac{Q_{\text{in}}}{Q_{\text{out}}} = \frac{1}{3}.$$

* **Tetrahedron** ($\{3, 3\}$, self-dual):

  $$\frac{r_{\text{in}}}{R_{\text{out}}} = \frac{1}{3} \approx 0.33333, \qquad \frac{Q_{\text{in}}}{Q_{\text{out}}} = \frac{1}{9}.$$

* **Dodecahedron & Icosahedron** ($\{5, 3\}$ and $\{3, 5\}$ over $\mathbb{Q}(\phi)$):

  $$\frac{r_{\text{in}}}{R_{\text{out}}} = \sqrt{\frac{5 + 2\sqrt{5}}{15}} = \sqrt{\frac{4\phi + 3}{15}} \approx 0.79465, \qquad \frac{Q_{\text{in}}}{Q_{\text{out}}} = \frac{5 + 2\sqrt{5}}{15}.$$
