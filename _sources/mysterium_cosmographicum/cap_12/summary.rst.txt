Chapter XII: Summary
====================

Division of the Zodiac, Musical Concords, and the Two Treasures
---------------------------------------------------------------

1. **The 120-fold & 12-fold Zodiac**: Sectioning the five Platonic solids produces squares (Cube, Octahedron), triangles (Tetrahedron), and decagons (Dodecahedron, Icosahedron), whose inscribed arcs share a common unit of :math:`\frac{1}{120}` of the circle (:math:`3^\circ`, whose triple is :math:`360^\circ`). Using only the square and triangle yields the 12-fold Zodiac (:math:`30^\circ` signs).
2. **Seven Harmonic String Divisions**: Dividing a monochord string so that both parts sound consonant with the whole and each other yields exactly seven natural divisions—:math:`\frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{3}, \frac{1}{4}, \frac{1}{5}, \frac{1}{6}, \frac{2}{5}, \frac{3}{8}`—whose least common denominator is again :math:`120` (and :math:`12` for the three perfect concords alone).
3. **The Two Treasures of Geometry**: Kepler links the three primary solids (Cube, Tetrahedron, Octahedron) to the **Pythagorean Theorem** (*Massa Auri*, :just:`I.47`) and the perfect concords, while linking the secondary solids (Dodecahedron, Icosahedron) to the **Divine Section** (*Gemma*, :term:`sectio divina <sectio-divina>`, :just:`II.11`, :just:`VI.30`) and the imperfect concords.
4. **Planetary Aspects**: Wrapping the divided string into a circle aligns the octave, fifth, fourth, and soft third (*B mollis*) with opposition (☍), trine (△), quartile (□), and sextile (⚹), while proposing three new minor aspects (:math:`72^\circ`, :math:`144^\circ`, :math:`135^\circ`) and explaining geometrically why adjacent signs (:math:`30^\circ, 150^\circ`) form no aspect.
