Chapter XXI: Notes & Provenance
===============================

Why Kepler's 1596 Period Formula Systematically Overshot Small Gaps
-------------------------------------------------------------------

Look at Kepler's table on folio ``0087`` (p. 75) through modern eyes! Kepler's 1596 algebraic rule effectively used :math:`r_2/r_1 \approx \frac{1}{2}(1 + T_2/T_1)` (arithmetic mean of :math:`1` and the period ratio), whereas the true :term:`Third Law <lex-tertia>` is :math:`(T_2/T_1)^{2/3}`. For ratios near :math:`1` (Mars–Earth, Earth–Venus), :math:`T_1/T_2` is close to :math:`1` and the linear approximation systematically overestimates the smaller inner ratio :math:`r_1/r_2`—explaining the exact positive sign pattern (``+36``, ``+43``) that Kepler astutely noticed in 1596!

Two minor 1596 *editio princeps* printing quirks are preserved verbatim in the text: on folio ``0087`` (p. 75), the Mars/Earth difference is printed ``+26`` (arithmetically ``694 − 658 = +36``); and on folio ``0088`` (p. 76), the lowest simple Earth distance is printed ``858 e`` in the left column while the proportional calculation at right uses ``e 958`` (``1000 − 42 = 958``, yielding ``762 f``).
