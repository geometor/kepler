Chapter XXIII: Astronomical Beginning & End of the World
========================================================

.. folio:: mysterium_cosmographicum:0092
   :page: p. 80

.. folio:: mysterium_cosmographicum:0095
   :page: p. 83 (Closing Hymn & FINIS)

*"After the banquet, let us come to the dessert (bellaria)."* Closing the *Mysterium Cosmographicum* with two grand cosmological problems, Kepler examines the **astronomical beginning** of planetary motion (the configuration of the heavens at Creation) and its **astronomical end**—the ancient doctrine of the **Platonic Great Year** (*Annus Platonicus*), when all planets supposedly return simultaneously to their initial conjunction.

Using the Prutenic Tables to compute backward to the epoch of Creation (testing both vernal Aries $0^\circ$ on April 27 and an autumnal Libra configuration, tabulated on folio ``0092``, p. 80), Kepler critiques Scaliger's chronological assumption of a New Moon at Creation—noting that the Moon, appointed to rule the night, should shine full on the first night.

Far more mathematically profound is Kepler's decisive **refutation of eternal recurrence** (the *Annus Platonicus*): if the orbital periods of the six planets stand in ratios governed by the **irrational geometric proportions** of the five regular solids (involving $\sqrt{2}$, $\sqrt{3}$, and the golden irrational $\sqrt{5}$), then planetary revolutions are **incommensurable**! Consequently, the planets will **never** return together to the exact configuration from which God first set them in motion—even across infinite time—proving that cosmic history is linear and unrepeatable rather than cyclic.

Kepler seals his youthful masterwork on folio ``0095`` (p. 83) with an exalted Latin hexameter paraphrase of **Psalm 8** (*Maxima Mundi Opifex...*), marveling that the Creator crowns humble humanity with intellect capable of comprehending the vast celestial orbs above—closing with **FINIS.**
