Chapter IX: Planetary Qualities Derived from the Five Solids
============================================================

.. folio:: mysterium_cosmographicum:0043
   :page: p. 33

Having established the spatial sequence of the five regular solids, Kepler briefly explores whether traditional planetary qualities and mutual affinities in physical astrology reflect the geometric natures of their assigned bodies. Assigning to each exterior planet the solid inscribed *within* its sphere and to each interior planet the solid *circumscribing* it—leaving Earth solely as boundary (*limes*) and setting Sun and Moon apart as luminaries—yields a clean one-to-one correspondence between the five regular bodies and the five wandering stars:

* **Saturn** $\leftrightarrow$ **Cube**: As the Cube measures all other solids by its unyielding right angles, Saturn imparts rigidity, constancy, and exact measure.
* **Jupiter** $\leftrightarrow$ **Tetrahedron** (*Pyramis*): Standing as a benefic mediator between two harsh neighbors (Saturn and Mars), the Pyramid stands geometrically between Cube and Dodecahedron, rigid purely by its triangular edges.
* **Mars** $\leftrightarrow$ **Dodecahedron**: Combining many edges with fewer faces and non-rigid pentagonal angles, Mars pairs with Saturn's non-rigid square faces in traditional antipathy, while contrasting with Venus's greater face multiplicity.
* **Venus** $\leftrightarrow$ **Icosahedron**: Possessing the greatest number of faces (20) and highest rotational grace among the five bodies, the Icosahedron mirrors Venus's fecundity and versatility.
* **Mercury** $\leftrightarrow$ **Octahedron**: Pivoting on opposite vertices with four continuous equatorial edges passing cleanly around its middle, the Octahedron reflects Mercury's swift mobility and mediating versatility—a unique equatorial property Kepler will later exploit mathematically in Chapter XVII.

Kepler emphasizes that whether or not readers endorse every astrological analogy, the geometric symmetries of angular rigidity, face multiplicity, and vertex mobility mirror classical planetary affinities with uncanny fidelity.
