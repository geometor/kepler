Chapter XIV: Summary
====================

Astronomical Proof of the Five-Solid Intervals (Tabella IIII)
-------------------------------------------------------------

Kepler refutes the Aristotelian-scholastic assumption that celestial spheres are packed seamlessly without intervening space. Under :person:`Copernicus's <copernicus>` heliocentric distances, vast empty intervals separate every pair of neighboring planetary shells—even between Earth and Venus, which remain at least $12$ units apart (in units where Earth's mean radius is $60$) even at closest approach.

Displaying the true orbital thicknesses and gaps to scale in **Tabella IIII** (``0059``), Kepler compares the inner-to-outer shell ratios predicted by the five Platonic solids ($577, 333, 795, 795, 577/707$) directly against Copernicus's *De revolutionibus* Book V numbers ($635, 333, 757, 794, 723$, or $801$ and $847$ when Earth's shell includes the Moon's orb). Mars ($333$ vs. $333$) and Venus ($795$ vs. $794$) match virtually to the digit, establishing that the Copernican orbital spacing cannot be accidental.
