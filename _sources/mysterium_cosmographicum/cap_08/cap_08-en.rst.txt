Chapter VIII: Why the Octahedron Lies Between Venus and Mercury
===============================================================

.. folio:: mysterium_cosmographicum:0042
   :page: p. 32

In this concise single-page chapter, Kepler resolves the ordering question raised in Chapter VII: why must the **Icosahedron** lie directly inside Earth's orb (between Earth and Venus) and the **Octahedron** occupy the innermost station between **Venus** and **Mercury**?

Kepler gives six architectural reasons proving that the two orders stand back-to-back at Earth, facing outward toward opposite extremes of the cosmos:

1. **Opposite poles of dignity**: Since the primary and secondary classes are genuinely distinct orders, their heads naturally face opposite cosmic poles.
2. **Circumference and Center**: Just as the **Cube** (head of the primary order) approaches the noblest exterior region—the circumference of the fixed stars—so the **Octahedron** (head of the secondary order) fittingly approaches the noblest interior locus—the **Center and Sun**.
3. **Symmetric framing**: Viewed as one unified edifice, the planetary architecture is bounded at both ends by its two chief dual partners—Cube outermost, Octahedron innermost.
4. **Gradual transition of face counts**: Moving outward from Earth's boundary in either direction steps gracefully from the two many-faced (*multifacia*) golden solids—the 12-faced Dodecahedron and 20-faced Icosahedron—downward toward simpler face counts (4 and 6 outside; 8 inside), rather than jumping abruptly from 12 to 8 and back up to 20.
5. **Affinity at the boundary**: Because the Dodecahedron closes the primary order, its own dual partner—the Icosahedron—fittingly meets it across Earth's orb.
6. **Honor to Earth**: Placing the two golden *multifacia* symmetrically on either side of Earth honors humanity's station as the central dividing wall (*maceries*).

Thus the wise Creator joined the two orders of the five solids **heel-to-heel at Earth**, sending their two royal heads outward to the fixed stars and inward to the Sun.
