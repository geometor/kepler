Pentamerous Blossoms & the Six-Cornered Snowflake: Summary¶

Botanical Pentamery & Fibonacci Convergence to \(\phi\)¶

Contrasting inanimate sixfold ice crystals with living five-petaled blossoms, Kepler connected Euclid’s self-propagating Divine Section (\(1 : \phi = \phi : (1 + \phi)\)) to the integer recurrence \(F_{n+1} = F_n + F_{n-1}\) (\(1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21\dots\)), observing both monotone rational convergence \(F_{n+1}/F_n \to \phi\) and the alternating overshoot/undershoot captured by Cassini’s identity \(F_{n+1}F_{n-1} - F_n^2 = (-1)^n\) (fol. 0034, p. 12).

Cold, Cubic Vapor Lattices & the Octahedral Hypothesis¶

Testing external cold as a purely privative cause, Kepler rejected wind-chink hoarfrost striations as a model for open-air snowfall and provisionally proposed that falling snowflakes begin as three orthogonal downy diameters—the skeleton of a regular octahedron (fols. 0035–0037, pp. 13–15). To test whether material necessity could generate these axes, he modeled equal vapor drops in a \(3 \times 3\) simple cubic contact lattice (0037.g1, p. 15), where each sphere is shielded from cold along four in-plane and two vertical contact points, before turning to the Earth’s facultas formatrix and cube–octahedron vertex–face duality (fols. 0038–0042, pp. 16–20).

Empirical Correction, Five Hexagonal Causes & Chemical Finale¶

In a decisive observational turn during a fresh snowfall (fol. 0043, p. 21), Kepler refuted his own three-dimensional octahedral model: snowflakes fall either as irregular non-symmetric granules or as truly flat planar six-cornered starlets (sometimes resting on a seventh vertical root-ray). Renewing the inquiry for planar symmetry (fols. 0044–0046, pp. 22–24), he weighed five hypotheses—non-closure into a solid, gapless plane tiling, closest tiler to the circle, sterile vs. fertile plant morphology, and mineralogical habitus—citing hexagonal quartz, Dresden ore dodecahedra, Boll fossil icosahedra, vitriol, and niter before handing the problem of salt in snow to the chemists and closing with Nihil sequitur / FINIS (fol. 0046, p. 24).
