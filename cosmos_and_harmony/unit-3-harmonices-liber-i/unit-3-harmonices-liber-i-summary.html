Core Epistemological & Algebraic Theses (Harmonices Mundi Book I, 1619)¶

Kepler’s Anticipation of Galois Multiplicity: In Proposition XLV, Kepler points out not only that Jost Bürgi's chord polynomial $y^6 - 7y^4 + 14y^2 - 7 = 0$ has cubic degree in $y^2$, but also that its three positive roots $2\sin(\pi/7), 2\sin(2\pi/7), 2\sin(3\pi/7)$ are algebraically indistinguishable conjugates over $\mathbb{Q}$—remarkably foreshadowing Galois-group symmetry two centuries before Galois and Wantzel (1837).
