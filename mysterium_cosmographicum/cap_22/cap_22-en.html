Chapter XXII: Why Planets Move Uniformly About the Equant Center¶

f. mysterium_cosmographicum:0090 · p. p. 78 (Solar Motive Force & Equant Diagram)

Returning to a subtle technical objection raised by Michael Mästlin concerning Chapter XIV, Kepler explains the physical origin of Ptolemy’s equant (and Copernicus’s equivalent small epicyclet): why does a planet move slower at aphelion and faster at perihelion, sweeping nearly uniform angles around an empty equant point $D$ placed twice as far from the Sun as the orbit’s geometric center $B$?

Mästlin had pointed out that Copernicus’s tiny epicyclet—introduced to reproduce the equant without breaking uniform circular motion—doubles the formal shell thickness of a planet’s orb (see Tabella IIII, 0059), which would crush the interstitial spaces for the five solids! Kepler replies first that solid shells do not exist (citing Chapter XVI and Tycho Brahe, “the noble and most excellent Danish mathematician”), so only the actual path swept by the planet’s globe matters.

Even more profoundly, Kepler shows on folio 0090 (p. 78) that his solar moving soul from Chapter XX directly produces the equant effect inside every single planetary orbit! In his engraved diagram on p. 78:

  • Point A is the source of the motive soul—the Sun (center of concentric circle cNOLM).

  • Point B is the geometric center of the eccentric planetary path cEFGH, with eccentricity AB.

  • Point D lies along the line of apsides at double distance $AD = 2\,AB$ (so BD = AB, midpoint C bisects $BD$, and cIKPQ is the equant circle).

At aphelion $EF$, the planet stands farther from the Sun A by distance AB than on the concentric circle cNOLM, so the weakened solar force slows it down; at perihelion $HG$, it approaches closer to A by AB, so the strengthened solar force accelerates it—making the planet’s speed vary inversely with solar distance exactly as if it moved uniformly about the bisected equant center D! Only Earth’s annual motion still puzzled Kepler here, because Copernicus and Ptolemy had given Earth no equant—a puzzle Kepler later cracked in Astronomia Nova (1609) by proving Earth does obey the exact same bisected eccentricity and Area Law!
