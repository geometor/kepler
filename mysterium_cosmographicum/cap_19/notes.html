Chapter XIX: Notes & Provenance¶

Mästlin’s Role in the 1596 Edition & The Venus Anomaly¶

Folios 0080–0081 (pp. 68–69) preserve Michael Mästlin's direct voice inside Kepler’s text—explaining why the Mercury column in Table V (Chapter XV; marginal note on p. 68: “Hi numeri sunt in Tabula V. quae est Cap. XV. ad ☿”) was computed for Ptolemy’s epoch rather than Copernicus’s, and confirming how closely teacher and pupil collaborated during the Tübingen printing. Historically, the \(2^\circ 18'\) residual on Venus proved real rather than observational: in Harmonices Mundi Book V (1619), Kepler finally explained why Venus’s aphelion/perihelion shell departs from the pure Icosahedron through the musical consonances of extremal angular velocities.

Textual & Typographical Notes on the 1596 Print¶

  • Running-Head Misprint (p. 67): Folio 0079 (p. 67) carries the mistaken running head CAPVT XVIII. in the 1596 editio princeps instead of CAPVT XIX..

  • Compositor’s Slip (p. 68): Folio 0080 (p. 68, line 10) prints ut proire capite where sense requires ut priore capite (“as in the preceding chapter”, referring back to Rheticus’s letter quoted in Chapter XVIII).

  • Greek Ligature (p. 68): Folio 0080 (p. 68, line 3) prints ἐναργέστατον using the Renaissance Tübingen στ ligature.

  • Saturn Observation (p. 66): The dual date die 2, 12 Novemb. anno 1594 and die 21, 31. Octobris records both Old Style (Julian) and New Style (Gregorian) calendar dates, separated by ten days in the late sixteenth century.

Transcription & Provenance¶

  • Primary Witness: Johannes Kepler, Prodromus Dissertationum Cosmographicarum, continens Mysterium Cosmographicum (Tübingen, 1596, Caput XIX, pp. 65–69 (folios 0077–0081); Editio altera cum notis authoris, Frankfurt, 1621; ed. Ch. Frisch, Vol. I, pp. 160–163).

  • Folio Scans: Verified 300-DPI scans mysterium_cosmographicum:0077–0081 (pp. 65–69).
