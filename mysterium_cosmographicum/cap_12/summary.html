Chapter XII: Summary¶

Division of the Zodiac, Musical Concords, and the Two Treasures¶

  1. The 120-fold & 12-fold Zodiac: Sectioning the five Platonic solids produces squares (Cube, Octahedron), triangles (Tetrahedron), and decagons (Dodecahedron, Icosahedron), whose inscribed arcs share a common unit of \(\frac{1}{120}\) of the circle (\(3^\circ\), whose triple is \(360^\circ\)). Using only the square and triangle yields the 12-fold Zodiac (\(30^\circ\) signs).

  2. Seven Harmonic String Divisions: Dividing a monochord string so that both parts sound consonant with the whole and each other yields exactly seven natural divisions—\(\frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{3}, \frac{1}{4}, \frac{1}{5}, \frac{1}{6}, \frac{2}{5}, \frac{3}{8}\)—whose least common denominator is again \(120\) (and \(12\) for the three perfect concords alone).

  3. The Two Treasures of Geometry: Kepler links the three primary solids (Cube, Tetrahedron, Octahedron) to the Pythagorean Theorem (Massa Auri, [I.47]) and the perfect concords, while linking the secondary solids (Dodecahedron, Icosahedron) to the Divine Section (Gemma, sectio divina, [II.11], [VI.30]) and the imperfect concords.

  4. Planetary Aspects: Wrapping the divided string into a circle aligns the octave, fifth, fourth, and soft third (B mollis) with opposition (☍), trine (△), quartile (□), and sextile (⚹), while proposing three new minor aspects (\(72^\circ\), \(144^\circ\), \(135^\circ\)) and explaining geometrically why adjacent signs (\(30^\circ, 150^\circ\)) form no aspect.
