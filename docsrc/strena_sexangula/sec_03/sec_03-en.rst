Pomegranate Seeds & Sphere Close-Packing
========================================

.. folio:: strena_sexangula:0031
   :page: p. 9

Turn next to the **pomegranate** (*malum granatum*), whose angular crimson kernels are packed tightly inside a tough rind. Why are those seeds faceted into rhomboidal planes? Not from any formal soul counting faces, declare I, but from **material compression of round globules**!

Suppose you gather equal round pellets on a single horizontal plane and press them together until they touch one another mutually: they coalesce either in **quadrangular** order (Figure **A**, where four neighbors touch one central pellet at $90^\circ$) or in **triangular** order (Figure **B**, where **six** pellets surround one central pellet along a regular hexagon with equal center-to-center quadrances $Q = 4R^2$).

Now stack such layers in three dimensions:

* Stacking square layers directly atop square layers yields simple cubic order, where each sphere is touched by **six** neighbors ($\eta_{\text{cubic}} = \frac{\pi}{6} \approx 52.36\%$).
* Seating each sphere of a square layer into the hollow pit of four spheres below—or equivalently seating each sphere of a triangular-hexagonal layer (tiers **A** through **E**, p. 10) into the hollow of three spheres below—yields **pyramidal close-packing**, where every interior sphere is kissed simultaneously by **twelve** neighbors (six in its own horizontal plane, three above, and three below)!

   *"The fitting together will become the **tightest possible**, so that **in no other arrangement whatsoever could more globules be packed into the same vessel**"* (*Coaptatio fiet arctissima, ut nullo praeterea ordine plures globuli in idem vas compingi queant* — the :term:`Kepler Conjecture <kepler-conjecture>`, $\eta_{\text{FCC}} = \frac{\pi}{3\sqrt{2}} \approx 74.048\%$).

And if uniform swelling or external pressure squeezes these twelve-kissed spheres until all interstitial voids vanish, each sphere deforms into an exact **Rhombic Dodecahedron**! Conversely, Kepler contrasts this twelve-kissed close packing with the orthogonal $3 \times 3$ cubic vapor-globule contact lattice (p. 15, folio ``0037``), where each globule touches only six neighbors along three perpendicular axes.
