Unit V · Historical & Mathematical Commentary
=============================================

1. Mästlin's 1597 Decimal `0.6180340` & the Quartic Kepler Field $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\phi})$
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

In October 1597, while corresponding with :person:`Michael Mästlin <maestlin>` (:cite:`Frisch I, p. 377 <kepler:1597:maestlin>`), :person:`Kepler <kepler>` posed the problem of constructing a right-angled triangle whose three sides form a continuous geometric progression $a : b : c$. Setting $a = 1$ and $b = \sqrt{c}$ forces $1 + c = c^2$, so $c = \phi = \frac{1 + \sqrt{5}}{2}$ and $b = \sqrt{\phi}$. On the letter itself, Mästlin wrote down for a hypotenuse of $10000000$ the greater segment $6180340$—historically the **first known decimal approximation of the inverse golden ratio** $\phi^{-1} \approx 0.6180339887\dots$, verified in ``geometor.kepler.harmonices_geometry.verify_maestlin_1597_decimal`` to have an exact error below $1.13 \times 10^{-8}$!

In ``geometor.model``, coordinates involving $\sqrt{\phi}$ live naturally in the degree-4 algebraic number field ``get_kepler_field()`` generated by $\alpha = \sqrt{\phi}$ with minimal polynomial

$$\alpha^4 - \alpha^2 - 1 = 0.$$

Yet in Rational Trigonometry (:term:`quadrance <quadrance>`), the squared side lengths $(1, \phi, \phi^2)$ descend cleanly to the quadratic golden field $\mathbb{Q}(\phi)$!

2. Angular Momentum & the Inverse-Square Ratio of Apsidal Angular Speeds
------------------------------------------------------------------------

In *Astronomia Nova* (:cite:`1609 <kepler:1609:an>`), Kepler discovered the Second Law (equal focal areas in equal times, $dA/dt = \frac{1}{2} r^2 \dot{\theta} = \text{const}$). At perihelion $r_{\text{per}} = a(1 - e)$ and aphelion $r_{\text{aph}} = a(1 + e)$, the velocity vector is perpendicular to the solar radius vector, so the heliocentric angular speeds $\omega = \dot{\theta}$ satisfy **strictly**

$$\frac{\omega_{\text{per}}}{\omega_{\text{aph}}} = \left(\frac{r_{\text{aph}}}{r_{\text{per}}}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{1 + e}{1 - e}\right)^2.$$

Solving $\left(\frac{1 + e}{1 - e}\right)^2 = \frac{q}{p}$ for a rational harmonic interval $p : q$ yields the algebraic eccentricity law $e = \frac{\sqrt{q} - \sqrt{p}}{\sqrt{q} + \sqrt{p}}$. For Mars ($p : q = 2 : 3$), this gives $e = \frac{\sqrt{3} - \sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{3} + \sqrt{2}} = 5 - 2\sqrt{6} \approx 0.1010$, remarkably close to Mars's true orbital eccentricity $e \approx 0.0934$!

3. From *Proportio Sesquialtera* (1618) to Universal Gravitation (1687) & the 1621 Second Edition
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Kepler's Third Law $(T_1 / T_2)^2 = (a_1 / a_2)^3$ (*Book V*, Ch. III, Prop. VIII) became the decisive quantitative touchstone from which Isaac Newton deduced in *Principia* (1687, Book I, Props. IV & XI) that a centripetal force holding planets in focal ellipses with $T^2 = \frac{4\pi^2}{GM} a^3$ must vary **inversely as the square of the distance** ($F \propto 1/r^2$). Two years after *Harmonices Mundi*, Kepler brought his lifework full circle by issuing the annotated second edition of *Mysterium Cosmographicum* (:cite:`Frankfurt 1621 <kepler:1621:mc2>`), leaving his youthful 1596 text intact while supplying candid *Notae Authoris* explaining where pure polyhedral geometry required completion by elliptical dynamics and celestial harmony.
