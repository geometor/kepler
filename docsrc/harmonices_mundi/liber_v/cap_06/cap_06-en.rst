Chapter VI: Musical Modes Expressed in the Extremes of Planetary Motions
========================================================================

.. folio:: harmonices_mundi:0298
   :page: p. 206

*Chapter VI. That musical Modes or Tones are expressed in a certain manner in the extremes of the planetary motions.*

This follows from what has already been said, nor is there need of many words; for individual planets mark out, in a certain manner, individual places of the scale, by traversing in their perihelial motion—so far as is granted to each—a certain interval in the Musical Scale, bounded by specific Keys or places of the System; beginning in each case from that Key or place which in the preceding chapter fell to its aphelial motion: namely to Saturn and the Earth *G*; to Jupiter *h* (which can be transposed higher onto *G*); to Mars *fe* [*f*]; to Venus *e*; and to Mercury *A* in the higher System. Behold each of them in the customary musical notes. They do not, indeed, form the intermediate places—which you see filled with notes here—articulately, as they do the extremes: because from one extreme they strive toward the opposite not by leaps and discrete intervals, but by a continuous tension,

.. folio:: harmonices_mundi:0299
   :page: p. 207

passing actually through all the intermediate pitches (infinite in potential): a thing which could not be expressed by me otherwise than through a continuous series of intermediate notes.

Yet the marking of two Keys in the common System, and the formation of the skeleton of an Octave through the spanning of a certain concinnous interval, is a kind of rudiment of the distinction of Tones or Modes: therefore musical Modes are distributed among the Planets. I know indeed that for the formation and definition of distinct Modes more things are required, which belong properly to human singing—inasmuch as it moves by discrete intervals—and therefore I have used the expression "in a certain manner."

Yet every harmonist will be free to put forward his own judgment as to which Mode each planet expresses most closely, once its extremes have here been assigned to it. For my part, out of the customary Modes I would assign the Seventh or Eighth to Saturn, because if you set its fundamental key at *G*, its perihelial motion ascends to *h*; to Jupiter the First or Second, because when its aphelial motion is fitted to *G*, its perihelial motion reaches *b*; to Mars the Fifth or Sixth, not merely because it nearly attains a Fifth (*Diapente*), which is an interval common to all Modes, but especially because, when reduced with the rest to the common system, it attains *c* in its perihelial motion and points toward *f* in its aphelial motion, which is the root of the Fifth or Sixth Tone or Mode; to the Earth I would assign the Third or Fourth, because its motions turn within a semitone, and the first interval of those Tones is indeed a semitone; to Mercury, because of the breadth of its interval, all Modes or Tones indifferently will be fitting; to Venus, because of the narrowness of its interval, plainly none—yet by reason of the common System, also the Third and Fourth, because with respect to the rest it holds *e*.

.. admonition:: Marginal Note (fol. 0299, p. 207)

   The Earth sings *MI FA MI*, so that even from the syllable you may conjecture that *MI*\ sery and *FA*\ mine hold sway in this abode of ours.
