Chapter IV: Summary
===================

Establishes Kepler's inverse-square extremal angular-speed theorem :math:`\omega_{\text{per}}/\omega_{\text{aph}} = ((1+e)/(1-e))^2` and defines the harmonic comparison between adjacent planets' converging and diverging speeds. Because a planet sweeps equal focal sector areas around the Sun (:g:`Sol_f`) in equal times—comparing the short, wide perihelion sector :g:`SEC_PER` against the long, narrow aphelion sector :g:`SEC_APH`—the apparent daily heliocentric angular motions (:math:`\omega`) at aphelion (:g:`A_aph`) and perihelion (:g:`P_per`) stand strictly in the inverse square of the extreme solar distances:

.. math::

   \frac{\omega_{\text{per}}}{\omega_{\text{aph}}} = \left(\frac{r_{\text{aph}}}{r_{\text{per}}}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{1 + e}{1 - e}\right)^2.

Comparing both converging and diverging extremal speeds of adjacent planets reveals pure harmonic ratios connecting every orbital boundary from Saturn down to Mercury.
