Chapter III, Prop. VIII: Summary
================================

Kepler's Third Harmonic Law (:math:`T^2 = a^3`)
-----------------------------------------------

In *Harmonices Mundi* Book V, Chapter III, Proposition VIII—conceived on March 8, 1618, initially rejected after a calculation slip, and triumphantly recovered on **May 15, 1618**—Kepler formulates the universal mathematical law (:term:`Lex Tertia <lex-tertia>`) linking planetary orbital periods to mean heliocentric distances: the squares of the periodic times are proportional to the cubes of the semi-major axes:

.. math::

   \left(\frac{T_1}{T_2}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{a_1}{a_2}\right)^3, \qquad \text{or} \qquad T \propto a^{3/2}.

Thus if one planet were four times farther from the Sun than another (:math:`a_1 : a_2 = 4 : 1`), its periodic time would be eightfold (:math:`T_1 : T_2 = 8 : 1`, since :math:`8^2 = 4^3 = 64`); if nine times farther (:math:`9 : 1`), twenty-sevenfold (:math:`27 : 1`, since :math:`27^2 = 9^3 = 729`). Returning after twenty-two years to the **five regular solids** of *Mysterium Cosmographicum* (:cite:`1596 <kepler:1596:mc>`), armed with :person:`Tycho Brahe's <brahe>` observations and the focal ellipse of *Astronomia Nova* (:cite:`1609 <kepler:1609:an>`), Kepler bridges polyhedral spacing and dynamical periods in preparation for the harmonic eccentricity proofs of Chapter IX.
