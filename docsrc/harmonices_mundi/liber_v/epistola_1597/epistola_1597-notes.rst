Letter to Mästlin: Notes & Provenance
=====================================

Mästlin's 1597 Decimal `0.6180340` & the Quartic Kepler Field :math:`\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\phi})`
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

In October 1597, while corresponding with :person:`Michael Mästlin <maestlin>` (:cite:`Frisch I, p. 377 <kepler:1597:maestlin>`), :person:`Kepler <kepler>` posed the problem of constructing a right-angled triangle whose three sides form a continuous geometric progression :math:`a : b : c`. Setting :math:`a = 1` and :math:`b = \sqrt{c}` forces :math:`1 + c = c^2`, so :math:`c = \phi = \frac{1 + \sqrt{5}}{2}` and :math:`b = \sqrt{\phi}`. On the letter itself, Mästlin wrote down for a hypotenuse of :math:`10000000` the greater segment :math:`6180340`—historically the **first known decimal approximation of the inverse golden ratio** :math:`\phi^{-1} \approx 0.6180339887\dots`, verified in ``geometor.kepler.harmonices_geometry.verify_maestlin_1597_decimal`` to have an exact error below :math:`1.13 \times 10^{-8}`!

In ``geometor.model``, coordinates involving :math:`\sqrt{\phi}` live naturally in the degree-4 algebraic number field ``get_kepler_field()`` generated by :math:`\alpha = \sqrt{\phi}` with minimal polynomial

.. math::

   \alpha^4 - \alpha^2 - 1 = 0.

Yet in Rational Trigonometry (:term:`quadrance <quadrance>`), the squared side lengths :math:`(1, \phi, \phi^2)` descend cleanly to the quadratic golden field :math:`\mathbb{Q}(\phi)`!
