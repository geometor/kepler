Chapter XII: Adulterate (Comma-Impure) Consonances
==================================================

.. folio:: harmonices_mundi:0150
   :page: p. 60

**Chapter XII.**

*On adulterate consonances.*

From the continuation of two octaves result imperfect consonances. For at the juncture of the two, two major tones :math:`8:9` follow in succession between *f* and *g* and between *g* and *a*. Therefore in *f–a* the interval is :math:`64:81`, which is greater by one comma :math:`80:81` than the just Major Third :math:`4:5`, or :math:`64:80`. Let us call this, with Galilei, a *ditone*.

The same interval is between *e* and *ge*, because between *e–f* and *ge–a* on both sides there is the same interval of a semitone.

Thus between *f* and *bb*, a semitone added to the abundant ditone *f–a* makes the interval :math:`60:81`, or :math:`20:27`, which is greater by a comma :math:`80:81` than one fourth :math:`3:4`, or :math:`60:80`.

Judge the same of *e–a*, because just as *e* is lower than *f* by a semitone, so also is *a* lower than *b*.

If these imperfect or abundant consonances are subtracted from the interval of an octave, they leave imperfect remainders deficient by one comma. For between *a* and *ff* of one octave there was the same :math:`81:128`, a deficient minor sixth.

The same between *ge* and *ee*, if you subtract *e–ge* from the octave.

Thus between *bb* and *ff* of one octave, after subtracting the imperfect fourth *f–bb* (:math:`20:27`) from the octave, there will remain the diminished fifth :math:`27:40`, that is, :math:`81:120`.

The same between *a* and *ee*, when *e–a* is subtracted from the octave.

But when the abundant ditone *f–a* is added to the minor third, etc. (:math:`5:6`), there is produced :math:`160:243`, the fifth (*diapente*) *f–cc*, abundant by one comma; for :math:`160:240` is :math:`2:3`, and :math:`240:243` is :math:`80:81`.

And when the abundant fifth *f–cc* is taken away from the interval of an octave, there remains the fourth *cc–f* (:math:`243:320`), deficient by one comma.

**Table of the six imperfect consonances.**

* In place of :math:`4:5` is :math:`64:81`, abundant.
* In place of :math:`3:4` [printed :math:`3:8`] is :math:`60:81` (or :math:`20:27`) abundant, and :math:`243:320` deficient.
* In place of :math:`5:8` is :math:`81:128`, deficient.
* In place of :math:`2:3` is :math:`81:120` (or :math:`27:40`) deficient, and :math:`160:243` abundant.

Nor yet on this account is there any need for the Platonic semitone or Limma :math:`243:256`, which he called a *limma* for a different reason than I do. For when two major tones—namely the ditone :math:`64:81`—were subtracted from the interval :math:`3:4`, this limma :math:`243:256` remained to him in place of a semitone. To me, however, there is no need of this subtraction; rather, my interval :math:`3:4` is increased and becomes :math:`20:27`.
