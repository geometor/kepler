Chapter V: Summary
==================

Chapter V demonstrates how each primary consonance decomposes naturally into chains of melodic (*concinnous*) steps, grounding traditional interval nomenclature in 5-limit Just Intonation:

* **Fourth (*Diatessaron***, :math:`3:4`): Divides into one major tone, one minor tone, and one diatonic semitone (:math:`\frac{8}{9} \cdot \frac{9}{10} \cdot \frac{15}{16} = \frac{3}{4}`), spanning four voices.
* **Fifth (*Diapente***, :math:`2:3`): Adds one major tone (:math:`8:9`) to the Fourth; one intermediate step remains undivided until Book V's planetary motions supply it.
* **Thirds & Sixths (*Mollia* vs. *Dura*)**: :math:`5:6` and :math:`5:8` derive their softer character from the expressible hexagon and octave bisection, while :math:`4:5` and :math:`3:5` inherit the harsher inexpressible pentagon.
* **Octave (*Diapason**, :*math:`1:2`): Joins Fifth and Fourth around a shared central tone into seven concinnous steps across eight voices, verifying* :math:`(9/8)^3 (10/9)^2 (16/15)^2 = 2` *and excluding the* Diesis (:math:`24:25`) *and* Limma* (:math:`128:135`) from the principal diatonic scale.
