Proposition IV: Circumscription of Regular Figures
==================================================

.. folio:: harmonices_mundi:0024
   :page: p. 7

**All Regular figures can simultaneously stand with all their angles upon one and the same circle.**

For by proposition 21 of the Third Book of Euclid, all equal angles can be inscribed in the same—and thus also in equal—segments of the same circle; but all the angles of a Regular figure are equal; therefore all the angles of one figure can be inscribed in equal segments of one circle. Moreover, once one angle is inscribed, all must actually be inscribed as well. For all the sides are equal, wherefore the segments of the circle that are cut off by pairs of sides of one angle are also equal, by proposition 24 of the Third Book of Euclid. Therefore both the angle and the extremities of the sides meet together upon the same circle. But the extremities of the sides are themselves the angles. It would be otherwise if, though the angles were equal, the sides were not equal; for then the necessity of inscribing all of them would be dissolved.
