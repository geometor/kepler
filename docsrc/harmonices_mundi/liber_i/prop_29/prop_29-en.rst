Proposition XXIX: Proportional Division of a Mizon into Its Elements
====================================================================

.. folio:: harmonices_mundi:0036
   :page: p. 18

**When any Mizon—whose square is equal to the rectangle under a length compounded of the proposed expressible line and a line potent in five quarters of it, and under a breadth potent in five quarters—is cut proportionally, then the lesser part becomes an Elasson (where Elasson is a name not of comparison, but of quality), while the greater part becomes another Mizon, again understood qualitatively, whatever its Elements may be.**

Let $GA$ be, as before (in the diagram on fol. 16), half the length of the proposed expressible line, and $AM$ half of it in turn, so that of the units in power of which $GA$ is potent in $4$, $AM$ is potent in $1$, and let $\angle GAM$ be right; $MG$ will therefore be potent in $5$ such units. Let $MA$ be continued on both sides, and with center $M$ and interval $MG$, let the semicircle $PGX$ be described. Therefore $PX$ is double $GM$; wherefore $PX$ will also be potent in five fourth parts of the square of the proposed line, the double of $GA$.
But the squares of $PG$ and $GX$ joined together are equal to the square of $PX$; therefore they too are five fourths of the square of the proposed expressible line. Further, if out of $PG$ and $GX$ you make one straight line, its square will consist of the two squares of $PG$ and $GX$, and of the two Rectangles under $PG$ and $GX$, to which are equal two rectangles under $GA$ and $PX$—that is, one Rectangle under the proposed line, double $GA$, and under $PX$, two lines expressible, yet commensurable in power only: for which reason this rectangle will be a Meson, by Euclid X.22.
Since therefore the square of the whole line $PGX$ consists of the expressible square of $PX$ and a Meson rectangle of the same breadth $PX$, these two—the square of $PX$ and the rectangle under double $GA$ and $PX$—are equal to the rectangle contained under the expressible line $PX$ and under the line compounded of $PX$ and double $GA$, lines commensurable in power only, of which parts the greater $PX$ exceeds the lesser (double $GA$) in power by a line commensurable with itself in length (for $PX$ is potent in $5$ of the units in which double $GA$ is potent in $4$;
the excess $1$ is therefore the square of a line which is incommensurable with $PX$, because $1$ is not to $5$ as a square number to a square); for which reasons the said line compounded of $PX$ and double $GA$ is a Binomial of the fourth species: since, I say, the square of the whole $PGX$ is equal to such a rectangle under a fourth Apotome and an Expressible line, the whole line $PGX$ will therefore be a Mizon. 
The Elements composing it are the parts $PG$ and $GX$. For since $PA$ is an Apotome and $AX$ is a Binomial, they are therefore incommensurable with one another in length. But as $PA$ is to $AX$, so is the square of $PG$ to the square of $GX$. Therefore $PG$ and $GX$ are incommensurable with one another in power, and thus simply; and they make the sum of their squares expressible, namely equal to the square of $PX$, while the rectangle under $PG$ and $GX$ is a Meson. 
Therefore, by Euclid X.39, the line compounded of $PG$ and $GX$ is a Mizon; and by X.76, when $PG$ is taken away from $GX$, an Elasson is left. Now the whole $PGX$ is cut proportionally at $G$; for as $PA$ is to $AG$, so is $PG$ to $XG$. But $PA$ is the greater part $OA$ of $GA$ cut proportionally, because $MP$ is potent in the quintuple of $MA$, and the Apotome $AP$ is equal to $AO$ by Euclid II.11. 
Therefore $PG$ is also the greater part of $GX$ cut proportionally; and by Euclid XIII.5, when $PG$ (the greater part) is added to the whole $GX$, it produces a new whole $PGX$ cut proportionally at $G$, so that now $PG$ is the lesser part of this compounded line, and $GX$ its greater part. And thus $PGX$, being a certain Mizon, is cut at the same point $G$ both into its Elements, from which a Mizon is named, and at the same time into its parts of the divine proportion.

.. folio:: harmonices_mundi:0037
   :page: p. 19

I say that these same parts of the proportionally cut line are at the same time also an Elasson and a Mizon. For since $AP$ is a fourth Apotome, therefore what is contained under the Apotome $AP$ and the Expressible $PX$ is the square of an Elasson, by Euclid X.94; and since $AX$ is a fourth Binomial, what is contained under this and the Expressible $PX$ is the square of a Mizon: 
but the squares of $PG$ and $GX$ are equal to the Rectangles $APX$ and $AXP$, each to each; therefore $PG$ is an Elasson, and $GX$ a Mizon.

Here therefore the names of qualities and the names of proportions meet together into one. For $PG$ is called the lesser part, with respect to the whole $PGX$ proportionally cut at $G$; it is also called the lesser line or lesser Element of the whole $PGX$, inasmuch as this is a certain Mizon qualitatively; 
finally it is called in Greek Elasson, which in Latin sounds as *minor*, qualitatively, with respect to two other lines not here expressed, by the subtraction of one of which from the other it is itself constituted.

In the same way $GX$ is called, first, the greater part of the whole $PGX$ proportionally cut; secondly, it is called the greater line or Element of the whole line $PGX$, as this is qualitatively a Mizon in its own proper right, no less than the whole $PGX$ in its own; 
but the lines making $GX$ itself a Mizon by their composition are not here expressed.

On account of this concurrence of the proportional section and the section of a Mizon into its Elements, I believe the qualitative Names of Mizon and Elasson were given to these species.

Here, however, we must take diligent care not to confuse the distinctions of things: proportional section is an absolute proportion, not bound to any single line first in knowledge, which is called the proposed Expressible line; whereas those species of Mizon and Elasson 
are figured by certain degrees of their departure from the first proposed Expressible line. Therefore the divine section proceeds to infinity; yet the property of Mizon and Elasson does not follow it: in the former (the section), the part which just now was greater becomes at the next step the lesser; in the latter, an Elasson by its quality never and in no respect becomes a Mizon, nor a Mizon an Elasson. Therefore, if the Mizon $GX$ be divided proportionally again, its greater part will be equal to $PG$, 
and thereby it will remain an Elasson qualitatively; by no means, however, will it become a Mizon in quality, though it becomes the greater part in quantity, so long at least as $GA$ is the proposed Expressible line.

You ask: if $PGX$ is a Mizon in quality, and $GX$ is also a Mizon in quality, why cannot the greater Element of $GX$ itself also be some Mizon, just as the greater Element of the Mizon $PGX$ was $GX$, itself also a Mizon? 
Because although both are a Mizon, both $PGX$ and $GX$, yet the formation in the former case is one thing, and in the latter another. For into the square of $PGX$ enters the whole square of $PX$, and the whole rectangle under double $GA$ and $PX$. But into the square of $GX$ enters half of the square of $PX$, namely that under $MX$ and $XP$, while of the rectangle under double $GA$ and $PG$ only the fourth part, namely that under $AM$ and $PX$. 
Here therefore the proportion of the Meson to the Expressible is one thing, and there another. Now our proposition endeavors to demonstrate this concurrence of the divine section and the qualitative composition in the same parts of the line concerning the former $PGX$ alone, and its own proportion of Meson to Expressible, and not likewise concerning the latter.

Note this also for the perfection of the analogy: that just as the Mizon $GX$, by the composition of the divine proportion, becomes a greater Mizon, namely $PGX$, when $PG$—which is the greater part of $GX$ in the divine section—is added;

so conversely, the Elasson $PG$ of this species, by the section of the divine proportion, gives $PY$, a lesser Elasson than itself—namely, the greater part of $PG$ cut, or the lesser part $GV$ of $GX$ cut: so that as the greatest $PGX$ falls by the divine section into the Mizon $XG$ and the Elasson $GP$, so the second Mizon $GX$ falls into two Elassons $XV$ and $VG$, equal namely to $GP$ and $PY$; and thus two Elassons compose one Mizon, while a Mizon and an Elasson compose another Greater Mizon.
