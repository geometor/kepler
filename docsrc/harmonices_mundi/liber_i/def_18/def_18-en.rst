Definition XVIII: Sixth Degree of Knowledge
===========================================

.. folio:: harmonices_mundi:0028
   :page: p. 11

The **sixth and lower degree of knowledge** is when two joined lines—neither of which is expressible nor *Mese* together, and which are also incommensurable with one another—make at least one of the two effects Expressible, while the other is *Meson*. And this degree is twofold: for either the sum of the squares is expressible and the Rectangle is *Meson*; or the former is *Meson* and the latter is expressible.

In the former Effect they resemble Two lines Expressible and commensurable in power only. For both being expressible in power—that is, their squares being Expressible—they also make the sum of both Expressible; whereas their Rectangle is *Meson*, by proposition 22 of the tenth book of Euclid.

In the latter effect they resemble two *Mesae* commensurable in power only, which stand to one another as two Expressible lines between which the first (from 2 *Mesae*) is a Mean proportional, by propositions 26 and 28 of the tenth book of Euclid. For because they are commensurable in power, by adding their powers they make a sum commensurable in power. Yet the parts are *Mesae*, and whatever is commensurable with a *Meson* is itself also *Meson*, by proposition 24 of the tenth book of Euclid.

.. folio:: harmonices_mundi:0030
   :page: p. 12

Here we indeed measure the Rectangle of the pair by the square plane of the diameter, yet not the sum of their squares as well: for to that sum we merely find two lines forming a Rectangle equal to it, whose squares we may measure by the square of the diameter.
