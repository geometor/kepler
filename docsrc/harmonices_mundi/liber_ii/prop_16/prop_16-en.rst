Proposition XVI: Sum of Congruent Angles in the Plane and in a Solid
====================================================================

.. folio:: harmonices_mundi:0068
   :page: p. 50

**Proposition XVI.**

*The sum of angles congruent in a plane is always four right angles, never greater; the sum of those congruent in a solid is less than this sum.*

For in a plane no more than four right angles surround a single point; therefore when the sum equals four right angles, no gap is left, and by Definition I the congruence is then in a plane. If the angles pave a plane, they do not rise up to solidity. And conversely, if angles fitted together in a plane leave a gap—when, namely, they are less than four right angles—

.. folio:: harmonices_mundi:0069
   :page: p. 51

then, when the two sides bordering the gap are drawn together and the gap is shut out, it is necessary that the angle rise up and become solid. In figure *H* of the copperplate on the following folio 53, three pentagons are depicted laid flat in the plane and gaping.
