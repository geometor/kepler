Proposition XXVI: The Two Regular Star Polyhedra
================================================

.. folio:: harmonices_mundi:0084
   :page: p. 60

**To the most perfect regular congruences may also be added two other congruences, formed from twelve plane pentagonal stars; and two semi-solid congruences, formed from octagonal and decagonal stars.**

For the pentagonal stars (:term:`stella quinquangula <stella-quinquangula>`, $\{5/2\}$) enclose solid figures spiny on all sides: one is formed with **twelve five-lined solid angles** (the **Small Stellated Dodecahedron**, $\{5/2, 5\}$, Plate ``Ss``), and the other with **twenty three-lined solid angles** (the **Great Stellated Dodecahedron**, $\{5/2, 3\}$, Plate ``Tt``). The former rests on three points, the latter on five at once; the former rises more gracefully upon an apex, while the latter sits more squarely resting upon five.

Although externally no single regular plane appears, but in its place an isosceles pentagonal triangle, yet five such triangles always lie together in one and the same plane, surrounding a hidden pentagon inside the solid as its heart, and with it forming the pentagonal star—called in German *Drudenfuß* (*pedem Truttae*, Paracelsus's sign of health). So close is the kinship of these two figures with the Dodecahedron and Icosahedron that the convex solids—especially the Dodecahedron—appear somewhat truncated and mutilated when compared with these spiny stars.
