Definitiones — De Congruentia in Plano et in Solido
===================================================

*Public-domain Neo-Latin text from Ioannis Keppleri Harmonices Mundi Liber II: Architectonicus, seu ex Geometria Figurata, de Figurarum Regularium Congruentia in Plano vel Solido* (:cite:`Linz, 1619, pp. 47–66 <kepler:1619:hm>`; :cite:`ed. Ch. Frisch, Vol. V, pp. 126–144 <frisch:1864:v5>`).

.. rst-class:: latin-source

Libro Primo contemplati sumus figuras regulares planas singulas seorsim, quatenus ex inscriptione in circulum sunt :term:`scibiles <scibilitas>`; iam Libro Secundo **Architectonico** consideramus earum **sociabilitatem ac :term:`congruentiam <congruentia>`**, quaerentes quaenam figurae regulares et stellatae lateribus iunctis vel **planitiem continuam sine hiatu sternant**, vel **in solido coeuntes corpus undique clausum efforment**.

* **Congruentia in plano** perfecta dicitur, cum anguli figurarum circa quodlibet punctum commune **quatuor rectos** ($360^\circ$) implent, ordoque circumcirca sine repugnantia in infinitum continuari potest. **Congruentia in solido** fit, cum tres pluresve anguli plani minores simul quatuor rectis ($< 360^\circ$) angulum solidum claudunt.
