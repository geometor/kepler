Chapter II: Notes & Provenance
==============================

Nicholas of Cusa, Pythagoras & The Geometrical Trinity
------------------------------------------------------

* **Theological Geometry**: Kepler's theological geometry draws explicitly on :person:`Nicholas of Cusa's <cusanus>` *De Docta Ignorantia* (:cite:`1440 <cusanus:1440>`), where the infinite sphere is a symbol of the divine essence. For Kepler, the central Sun represents God the Father, the outer stellar sphere God the Son, and the intermediate celestial aura the Holy Spirit. Rectilinear solids act as the archetypal scaffolding through which divine ideas are instantiated in physical magnitude.
* **Pythagorean Precedent**: Kepler cites :person:`Pythagoras <pythagoras>` as his ancient forerunner in associating the five regular solids with cosmic structure (earth/cube, cosmos/icosahedron, fire/pyramid, air/octahedron, water/dodecahedron), noting that Pythagoras lacked only Copernican empirical astronomy to apply them quantitatively.
* **Dual Pairs in the Table (fol. 0035)**: The closing table from Euclid XIII.18 scholium explicitly records the equal inscribed-sphere radii of the dual pairs (**Cube** / **Octahedron**, and **Dodecahedron** / **Icosahedron**) when circumscribed by a unit sphere, leaving the **Tetrahedron** with the smallest inscribed sphere (ratio :math:`1:3`).
