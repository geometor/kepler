Chapter XXII: Why Planets Move Uniformly About the Equant Center
================================================================

.. folio:: mysterium_cosmographicum:0089
   :page: p. 77

*Why a planet moves uniformly upon the center of the equant.*

You have just learned, Reader, to recognize even imperfect things, whereby I fear the less that you will hiss off the stage this last and chilly catastrophe. I wished, however, to report it last, both because I hold it in the last place and because it coheres with the motions, nor can it be dispatched without Chapter XX, even though it properly belongs to Chapter XIIII, as you were advised there.

When I had submitted this figural proportion of the heavens to the judgment of :person:`Maestlin <maestlin>` *(In the margin: See Table IIII, Chapter XIIII.)*, he reminded me of the epicycles of the superior planets, which :person:`Copernicus <copernicus>` introduced in place of equants, and which produce for the orb a thickness twice as great as the planet's ascent and descent requires. And in the inferior planets indeed there are other motions by which the planet is carried up to the full height of that epicycle and descends to its full lowness, whence in them an eccentric of an eccentric was assumed by Copernicus in place of an eccentrepicycle; while in Mercury there is a certain peculiar diameter along which it approaches and recedes from the Sun. In like manner it sometimes stretches out much farther from the Sun than the Star ever does. He thought, therefore, that such thickness ought to be left to the orbs as suffices to demonstrate the motions. To which I answered, first, that the whole business would have to be abandoned if the orbs were made twice as thick, for too much would be taken away from the *prosthaphaereses*; secondly, that nothing is lost from the nobility of this wondrous contrivance, provided only that the paths themselves, traced out by the globes of the planets, retain this proportion—in whatever orbs they be conceived, whether great or small. And I added what you have in Chapter XVI concerning the matter of the figures, that there is none, and hence it is not absurd to enclose the bodies together with the orbs in the same place. Nay indeed, even without orbs this inequality of path can be defended. In which opinion I see the noble and most excellent mathematician :person:`Tycho Brahe <brahe>`, the Dane, is engaged. Yet these arguments of ours indicate the cause and manner more clearly. Namely, if the cause of slowness and swiftness in the orbs of individual planets be the same as it was above in Chapter XX in the whole universe, in this way: the eccentric path of a planet is slow above and swift below. For to demonstrate this, epicycles were assumed by Copernicus, and equants by :person:`Ptolemy <ptolemy>`. Let there be described, therefore, a concentric circle equal to the eccentric planetary path; its motion will be everywhere equal, because it is equally distant from the origin of motion. Therefore, in that half of the eccentric path which rises above the concentric, the planet will be slower, because it recedes farther from the Sun and is moved by a weaker power; in the remaining half it will be swifter, because nearer to the Sun and in a stronger power. And that this variation of motion is demonstrated through a small circle no otherwise than if the planet really moved with an equal motion upon that small circle, is easy for anyone to gather. You have the cause of this slowness; let us now see also the measure:

Let :g:`A` be the source of the moving soul, namely the Sun; :g:`B` the center of the path :g:`cEFGH` (``EFGH``), along which the planet advances, though with an unequal pace; let :g:`/ B D /` be as :g:`/ A B /`, and :g:`C` (``CB``) its half. Since, therefore, ``EF`` is farther from :g:`A` than ``NO`` (on :g:`cNOLM`) by the quantity :g:`/ A B /`, it was fitting that the planet in ``EF`` should be as slow as if it had receded twice as far from :g:`A`, namely by the quantity ``AD``, and ran upon the center :g:`D`. And conversely, since ``HG`` is nearer to :g:`A` than ``PQ`` by the same quantity :g:`/ A B /`, it was fitting that the planet in ``GH`` should be as swift as if it had approached twice as

   :page: p. 79

near to :g:`A`, indeed likewise by the quantity ``AD``. On both sides, therefore, it is just the same as if it advanced upon the center :g:`D`. For above in Chapter XX this was the proportion of the motions to the orbs. Wherefore consider that the two causes which there concurred throughout the whole circle are here inverted and intermingled. There, the greater and more remote whole circuit of the same orb increased the period, and the smaller and nearer one diminished it; here, however, the circles ``NOPQ`` (:g:`cNOLM`) and ``EFGH`` (:g:`cEFGH`) are equal, and one part of the latter is farther from, the other nearer to, the center :g:`A`, the Sun. Wherefore the motive power in :g:`A` acts upon ``EF`` and upon ``GH`` just as if the planet were there in ``IK`` and here in ``LM``. And the common measure of both—of that slowness and of this swiftness—is found in :g:`D`. Accordingly, the planet advancing along the path ``EFGH`` becomes slow and swift, and likewise middling around ``R`` and ``S``, just as if on ``IKLM`` (:g:`cIKPQ`) it went uniformly upon the center :g:`D`. Now behold the master astronomers, who have established the very same thing. Namely, :person:`Ptolemy <ptolemy>` made :g:`D` the center of the equant and :g:`B` the center of the planetary path. :person:`Copernicus <copernicus>`, however, leads around the center :g:`C`, midway between :g:`D` and :g:`B`, an eccentric of an eccentric or an eccentrepicycle. Thus for him it happens that the planet's path is as close as possible to ``EFGH``, while the equality of motion—just like that of the intermediate orb between ``EFGH`` and ``IKLM`` about :g:`C`—is regulated for the planet about :g:`D`.

You have the cause why the center of the equant is distant from the center of the eccentric by a third part of the whole eccentricity. Namely, let the whole universe be full of a soul which carries off whatever stars or comets it lays hold of, and that with the swiftness which the place's distance from the Sun and the strength of the power there require. Next, let there be in each planet a special soul, by whose rowing the star ascends in its circuit; and even when the orbs are removed, the same things will follow.

And when some read these things about the Equant, I know they will exult. For if astronomers marvel that Ptolemy assumed without demonstration this same measure of the Equant's center, some will now marvel much more that there was a cause for this matter, and yet Ptolemy had no inkling of it when he assumed the very thing just as it stands—and, as though blind, arrived at the right place by a divine nod.

Yet I would have them warned that nothing is blessed in every respect. For in Venus and Mercury that slowness and swiftness is accommodated not to the planet's digression from the Sun, but solely to the motion of the Earth. And if anyone should allege for this matter a different condition of motion

from the motion of the superior planets, what cause, finally, will he bring forward in the annual motion of the Earth? For that motion needed an Equant neither in Ptolemy nor in Copernicus. Wherefore let this uncertain dispute also remain pending before the astronomer as judge.
