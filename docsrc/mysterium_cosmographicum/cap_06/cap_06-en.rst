Chapter VI: The Tetrahedron Between Jupiter and Mars
====================================================

.. folio:: mysterium_cosmographicum:0040
   :page: p. 30

Now no one will greatly wonder why the Pyramid follows the Cube, since (1) it dares almost to contend with the Cube for primacy. (2) Moreover, either the pyramids themselves or analogous irregular ones serve for the composition of the remaining bodies. For 20 pyramids, slightly shorter than tetrahedral ones, compose the Icosahedron; 8 still shorter ones, the Octahedron. And although the Dodecahedron consists of a hidden square, it too must necessarily be resolved into pyramids. (3) Nor is this to be despised, that a Tetrahedron can be resolved into four complete pyramids and one Octahedron of sides smaller by half. (4) Just as in planes all polygons are resolved into triangles, so for the sake of measurement the remaining solids are resolved into pyramids, which we then measure by cubes, as we measure triangles by squares. Thus it is the measure of the rest, and is most easily measurable of all by the cube. (5) Hence most of its lines, like those of the cube, so readily receive their quantity from the ratio of the diagonal—yet not otherwise than in square numbers. (6) Moreover, the regularity of a pyramid depends upon its sides alone, whereas that of a cube depends also upon its angles. And thus among equilateral pyramids there is no more than one kind, whereas in a hexahedron, even with equal sides, there is an infinite variety of angles. On which account, if there were no other reasons, I leave in doubt whether it ought to be preferred to the cube or placed after it.

7. Imitating this ingenuity of nature, men first erect their timber by the plumb line and frame it at right angles, and then brace and stabilize it with triangles.

8. Furthermore, since the pyramid has an acute angle, it stands prior to the obtuse-angled bodies. For that is always first in order which has the just measure; what falls short of the just seems to follow next, because it appears both further removed from infinitude than what exceeds the just, and simpler as well. For an obtuse-angled figure seems in a certain way compounded out of a right angle and an acute one. Hence it is less to be wondered why the paucity of angles in its base, and even of the bases themselves, does not detract from the cube. For the number of angles and bases follows necessarily upon the adopted species of angle. Whence, if the right angle is prior to the acute, then the hexahedron too is prior to the Tetra-

hedron, and the four-angled body to the three-angled. 9. And this too may be gathered from the fact that everywhere the perfect comes first, next that which falls short, and last that which exceeds. Since, therefore, six is a perfect number of faces, it follows that the pyramid, which falls short, ought not indeed to precede the cube, yet ought immediately to follow it.

We have, then, why the Pyramid holds the second place between Jupiter and Mars. Above it was left in suspense which body holds the third place between Mars and the Earth. Yet that is easily decided here. For since the Dodecahedron is what remains from the primary bodies, it will be third in order, between Mars and the Earth; and what ought to be thought concerning its properties will easily become clear once a comparison with the preceding bodies is made.
