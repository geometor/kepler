Chapter VII: Order and Polar Duality of the Secondary Solids
============================================================

.. folio:: mysterium_cosmographicum:0041
   :page: p. 31

Turning to the **two secondary solids**—the **Octahedron** ($\{3, 4\}$) and the **Icosahedron** ($\{3, 5\}$)—Kepler resolves an apparent paradox: although the Octahedron is logically prior in nature to the Icosahedron, why does the **Icosahedron** stand higher in the heavens (between Earth and Venus) while the **Octahedron** stands lower (between Venus and Mercury)?

First, Kepler proves five ways in which the **Octahedron** is indeed the head (*caput*) of the secondary order, exactly mirroring the Cube's headship among the primaries:

1. **Lineage**: The Octahedron is "born" from the first two primary solids—borrowing its 4-fold vertex symmetry from the Cube and its triangular face from the Pyramid—whereas the Icosahedron descends from the Pyramid and the last primary solid, the Dodecahedron.
2. **Vertex cross-sections**: Viewed down a vertex, the four edges meeting at an Octahedron vertex outline the **square** base of the Cube, whereas the five edges around an Icosahedron vertex outline the **pentagonal** base of the Dodecahedron.
3. **Equal sphere-ratio height**: As Kepler will prove rigorously in Chapter XIII, the Octahedron and Cube have the **exact same insphere-to-circumsphere proportion** ($1/\sqrt{3}$), just as the Icosahedron and Dodecahedron share the exact same golden proportion ($\sqrt{(5+2\sqrt{5})/15}$).
4. **Vertex–face permutation (*permutatio*)**: The Cube's 6 faces and 8 vertices swap cleanly into the Octahedron's 6 vertices and 8 faces; likewise, the Dodecahedron's 12 faces and 20 vertices exchange identically with the Icosahedron's 12 vertices and 20 faces.
5. **Angular imitation**: The Octahedron imitates the Cube's right angle across its central square section, while the Icosahedron mirrors the Dodecahedron's obtuse angles.

Thus the Octahedron is indisputably chief of the secondary order—setting up Chapter VIII to explain why this very primacy draws the Octahedron closest to the central Sun.
