Algebraic & Harmonic Commentary
-------------------------------

* **Field Extensions**: Kepler's distinction between the two "treasures" anticipates the Euclidean constructibility tower: the Cube, Tetrahedron, and Octahedron reside in :math:`\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{3})`, whereas the Dodecahedron and Icosahedron require the golden field :math:`\mathbb{Q}(\phi) = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})` (:cite:`Pacioli 1509 <pacioli:1509>`).
* **Five New Aspects Foreshadowed**: Here in 1596 Kepler tentatively suggests testing :math:`72^\circ` (quintile, :math:`\frac{1}{5}`), :math:`144^\circ` (biquintile, :math:`\frac{2}{5}`), and :math:`135^\circ` (sesquiquadrate, :math:`\frac{3}{8}`) against meteorological records—a program he later formalized in *Harmonices Mundi* (1619) Book IV.
* **Plane Tiling & Aspect Geometry**: Kepler's fifth argument for why octagons and dodecagons do not form aspects invokes the Euclidean plane-tiling theorem (only triangles, squares, and hexagons tile the vertex neighborhood to :math:`360^\circ`), noting that rays operate via terrestrial vertex angles rather than celestial figures.
